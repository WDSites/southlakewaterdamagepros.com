Emergency Burst Pipe Water Damage Cleanup in Southlake, TX

A pressurized line keeps pouring until someone closes the main, so the first minutes matter most.

☎ Call (817) 623-9267

Water is running out of a broken pipe right now, and every minute it runs puts more of your house under water. Our dispatch line is open 24/7. While you wait for a crew, find the main water shutoff and turn it clockwise until the flow stops, then move rugs, shoes and boxes off the wet floor.

Burst pipe water damage in Southlake, TX is the harm caused when a pressurized supply line splits, a joint pulls apart or a fitting fails and water sprays or pours into the structure. Because the line is under pressure, the water does not just drip. It soaks insulation, travels inside walls and drops through ceilings.

This page covers what a burst line does inside a house, the order of work once the water is off, and how the dry-out is handled. If the pipe failed after a hard cold snap, the thaw side of the story is on our frozen pipe page.

Burst pipe in the house right now: what to do first

Stopping the supply comes before anything else. Most homes have a main shutoff near where the water line enters, often at a meter box outside, in a garage, or in a utility closet. Walk it out before you ever need it, because during a burst you will not want to search.

If the water is falling near a light fixture, outlet or ceiling fan, treat the area as live. Only switch off power at the breaker panel if you can reach it without stepping through water. If you cannot, leave the house and call the electric utility or 911 before you call us.

Once the main is closed, open a faucet at the lowest point in the house to drain what is left in the lines. Then take photos and video of the damage before anything is moved. Those pictures help your insurer, and they help the crew plan where to look.

What a split supply line leaves behind

The visible mess is often the smaller half of the problem. A burst line inside a wall can run for a long time before anyone notices, and the water follows studs, wiring holes and plumbing chases to places far from the break.

Use this table to match what you see with what is likely going on behind it.

Thermal imaging cameras and moisture meters show where water has settled, and the crew opens only what the readings say needs to open.

Safety steps for the first hour

Do not use a household vacuum on water, and do not run extension cords through wet areas. If the ceiling is bowed and heavy, stay out from under it. Poking a small hole to release the water can bring the whole panel down, so leave that for the crew.

Call a licensed plumber to find and repair the failed section. Our crews pull the water and dry the house, but the pipe itself belongs to a plumber. For city-side concerns, such as low pressure on the whole street or a problem at the meter, Southlake Public Works operations at 1950 E. Continental Blvd. can be reached at (817) 748-8082.

How we dry a house after a pipe lets go

Work starts once the water is off and the plumber has the line under control. Extraction units pull standing water from floors, and carpet and pad are lifted if they are saturated. For a larger pooled-water job, see our emergency water removal service.

Next comes the part most homeowners do not see. Small holes or flood cuts are made in drywall at the wet line so air and dry heat can reach the cavity. Insulation that has absorbed water is bagged out, because wet batts hold moisture against the framing for a long time.

Air movers go in to push dry air across surfaces, and LGR dehumidifiers pull the moisture out of the air so it cannot settle back into the walls. Moisture meters are checked on a schedule, and the equipment comes out only when the readings match dry reference material. The finished-drying handoff is what separates real water mitigation from a quick mop-up.

What changes the cost of a burst pipe job

No two burst pipe jobs cost the same, because the water does not behave the same way twice. These are the factors that move the scope.

  • How long the line ran before the main was closed.
  • Whether the water stayed clean (category 1) or sat long enough to turn gray (category 2).
  • How many rooms and floors the water reached, including ceilings below.
  • What materials got wet, such as hardwood, carpet and pad, drywall or insulation.
  • How many drying days and how much equipment the readings call for.
  • How much rebuild follows once the structure is dry.

Insurance and permits for a burst line

The Texas Department of Insurance says most homeowners policies cover water damage from leaks and broken pipes, with exceptions, and that home and renters policies cover leaks that happen inside the home. Check your policy for the exceptions and ask your insurer how your coverage treats your situation. We cannot promise approval of any claim.

The same agency warns that an insurer may deny a claim if you do not protect your property. That is why shutting off the main and moving valuables off the floor is not optional. Keep receipts for anything you do to limit the damage.

On the permit side, the City of Southlake permit guide lists replacing existing water or sewer lines as work that needs a permit, while swapping a fixture on existing plumbing does not. Ask your plumber who is pulling the permit, and ask the Southlake building inspections office at (817) 748-8236 if you are unsure.

Mistakes that make a burst pipe worse

The most common mistake is waiting. People mop, run fans for a day, and hope it was a small event, then find black staining at the baseboard a week later. Industry guidance such as ANSI/IICRC S500 is a published reference on water damage restoration, and you can ask any company whether its crews work to it.

  • Leaving the main open while waiting for the plumber.
  • Walking through rooms where the ceiling is holding water.
  • Tossing damaged items before taking photos.
  • Painting or patching over a wet wall.
  • Closing a wall before moisture readings are normal.
