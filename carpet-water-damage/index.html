Carpet Water Damage Restoration in Southlake, TX

Some wet carpet can be saved by drying in place, and some should go. We tell you which, and why, before work starts.

☎ Call (817) 623-9267

Carpet water damage happens when water soaks the carpet face, the backing and the cushion pad underneath, and the pad holds that water long after the top looks salvageable. If you are dealing with carpet water damage in Southlake, TX, this page explains how crews decide between drying carpet where it lies and pulling it out.

Southlake homes often have carpet over a wood subfloor or a concrete slab, upstairs in bedrooms and downstairs in family rooms. A supply line, a washer hose or an overflow tub can send water across a room in minutes, and the pad acts like a sponge.

If water is still standing, start with emergency water removal so it is pumped out first. This page covers what happens to the carpet and pad once the bulk of the water is gone.

What you see on wet carpet and what it tells us

Wet carpet gives away more than people realize. Where it feels, looks and smells different from the rest of the room, there is usually a reason hiding underneath.

What you see or feelWhat it usually meansLikely next step
Squishy sound underfootWater is sitting in the padExtraction, then pad inspection
Carpet lifting at the edgeAdhesive and tack strip loosened by moisturePull back a corner and check the subfloor
Ripples or bumps in the middleBacking stretched from waterPower stretch after drying, or replace if it will not hold
Brown stain at seams or edgesWicking from the pad or subfloorTest the source and the pad for contamination
Sour or musty odorDamp pad or contaminated waterRemove pad, treat subfloor, dry in place or replace

The first hour: safety steps for a wet carpeted room

Shut off the water at the source or at the main valve, and call a licensed plumber if a pipe or fixture failed. If the water is near outlets, a lamp or an appliance, leave and call the electric utility or 911 before touching anything.

Do not use a household vacuum on wet carpet, because that mixes electricity and water. Stay off the wet area, and lift furniture legs onto foil or wood blocks so dye and rust do not stain the carpet.

If the water came from a toilet, a drain or the street, keep people and pets out and do not try to clean it yourself. For city-side sewer concerns, Southlake Public Works takes calls at (817) 748-8082.

Clean, gray and black water: why category decides everything

Water from a broken supply line is clean at the start. After a day or two in a pad, it picks up bacteria and dirt, so even a clean-water loss gets less forgiving the longer it sits.

Gray water, such as a washing machine overflow, carries soap and some contamination. Black water, meaning sewage or rising ground water, contains harmful microbes, and porous material that touched it is removed, not cleaned.

ANSI/IICRC S500 is a published industry standard that sorts water into categories and describes how each is handled. Ask any company whether its crews work to it, and ask them to tell you the category they found.

When we dry carpet in place and when the pad comes out

Carpet face fibers are often synthetic and handle water well. The risk is the pad and the backing. Clean-water carpet that was reached quickly can often be dried where it lies using floating, a method that lifts carpet slightly so air moves under it.

Pad is a different story. It is a foam or fiber cushion that soaks up water and dries slowly, and in most losses it is cheaper and safer to replace the pad and keep the carpet.

These are starting points and not promises. The decision is confirmed with moisture meter readings and a look at the backing.

How we dry, clean and put the room back

Extraction comes first. A truck-mounted or portable extraction unit pulls water from the carpet and pad, followed by a pass with a weighted tool that squeezes more out.

  • Lifting or detaching a corner so the pad and subfloor can be checked.
  • Removing pad that cannot be saved and bagging it to keep the rest of the room clean.
  • Floating the carpet on air movers so air passes underneath.
  • Running LGR dehumidifiers to pull moisture out of the air and the subfloor.
  • Applying an antimicrobial to the subfloor and the back of the carpet where appropriate.
  • Re-stretching on the tack strip and installing a new pad once readings are normal.

The drying stage itself is described in more detail on our structural drying page. We do not rent equipment out, because the crew needs to place it and check the readings.

Seams, tack strip and odor: the details that matter

Seams are where carpet shows its age. When the seam tape gets wet, the adhesive can fail and the two pieces will pull apart, even if the carpet itself is fine. A reseam is part of putting the carpet back.

Tack strip is the wooden strip with small nails that holds carpet along the wall. It can swell and stain when wet, and wet strips can be replaced during reinstall.

Odor usually means moisture is still in the pad or subfloor. Covering it with spray does not solve it. The fix is to find the damp layer, dry it and remove anything that cannot be dried.

What changes the cost of carpet water damage

Carpet losses vary a lot from room to room. These are the factors that affect scope and price.

  • Water category: clean, gray or black water changes what can stay.
  • Square footage and how many rooms the water reached.
  • Pad type and whether the carpet can be reused or must be replaced.
  • Subfloor type, since wood and concrete dry differently.
  • Days of drying and the equipment needed to get to normal readings.

Insurance, flood policies and keeping evidence

Texas homeowner and renter policies often respond to sudden and accidental leaks inside the home, but they do not cover flood damage. According to the Texas Department of Insurance, flood damage needs a separate flood policy. Check your policy before you assume.

Photograph the carpet before anything is removed, and keep damaged items until the adjuster has seen them. If a section of carpet or pad has to be cut out for safety, save a sample and tell your insurer, then ask your insurer what else it wants to see.

Carpeting is on the City of Southlake's list of cosmetic work that does not need a permit. If the job also involves walls, plumbing or electrical work, ask the Southlake Building Inspections office at (817) 748-8236 whether a permit applies.

Mistakes that turn salvageable carpet into a total loss

Most carpet that gets thrown out could have been saved by acting sooner, or lost because of what was done in the meantime.

  • Waiting more than a day to call, so the pad and subfloor stay wet.
  • Walking on wet carpet and spreading dirt and dye.
  • Using a household vacuum on wet carpet.
  • Putting heavy furniture back before the room has been checked.
  • Laying new flooring or pad over a damp subfloor.
