Emergency Crawl Space Water Damage Cleanup in Southlake, TX

Water under a raised floor is easy to ignore and hard to reach, so it keeps soaking insulation and framing long after you notice it.

A crawl space is the shallow, unfinished area between the ground and the first floor of a raised house. This page matters only if your home has one, and not every Southlake home does, since some sit on slabs and some have basements. If yours does, water under the floor can reach insulation, joists and the subfloor above.

Standing water under your house right now

Start by finding out where the water is coming from. A leaking supply line, a drain pipe, a failed water heater connection or outside water pooling against the foundation each need a different fix. If it is a pipe, shutting off the main valve is the first and most useful move.

Then call dispatch. Crews bring submersible pumps and extraction equipment sized to a tight space, and they plan a safe route in before anything is plugged in. The water has to leave the cavity before drying can begin, and the longer insulation stays soaked, the more of it ends up discarded.

Signs the crawl space is wet before you ever look inside

You often learn about crawl space water from the living areas above it. Floors may feel springy or cold, hardwood can cup, and a musty smell tends to rise through vents and gaps along the baseboards.

Safety first: confined space, wiring and water

A crawl space is a confined space with low clearance, bad light and often wiring, ducts and a water heater feed in reach of wet ground. Do not enter alone, do not stand in water near live circuits, and do not use extension cords through the hatch.

  • Turn off the main water valve if a pipe is leaking.
  • Leave power alone near the hatch unless the panel is dry and easy to reach.
  • If you smell gas, leave the house and call 911 or the utility before calling us.
  • Call a licensed plumber for broken pipes and Southlake Public Works at (817) 748-8082 for water on the city side.
  • Take photos from the opening and keep damaged items for your adjuster.

Our process for drying under a floor system

Crews begin with extraction, pumping standing water and wet-vacuuming low spots in the soil or liner. Then they measure. Moisture meters read joists, the subfloor and sill plates, and thermal imaging cameras help show which insulation is holding water above.

Wet insulation is usually removed because it dries slowly and presses moisture against the wood. Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers are staged so air flows across framing rather than just across the soil. Compact HEPA air scrubbers may run when the crew opens damp materials, and readings are logged until the framing reaches its dry standard.

We do not install vapor barriers, encapsulation systems or drainage as part of this service. Those are separate trades, and restoration is not waterproofing. For the extraction stage on its own, see our page on emergency water extraction.

What changes the cost of crawl space cleanup

Access and depth drive crawl space jobs more than square footage does. These are the main cost factors.

  • Water category, because clean supply water and ground water are handled differently.
  • Access, including hatch size, clearance and how far crews must crawl.
  • How much insulation, liner or flooring has to come out.
  • Drying days and how many air movers and dehumidifiers the space needs.
  • Rebuild scope for subfloor, ducts or insulation afterward.

Drainage, flood maps and what the city covers

The city's own website says its Streets and Drainage Team responds to storm damage, but city crews are not legally allowed to use public resources to clean private property. Homeowners and HOAs maintain their own grounds, so grading, downspouts and yard drains are usually yours to fix. If water keeps returning after rain, the cause may sit outside the house.

The city's interactive map shows 100-year and 500-year flood zones under the Environmental tab. Check the city's flood zone map and the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for your address, since flood risk differs by location. If surface flooding is the cause, our flood damage restoration page covers that stage.

Pipes, insulation and winter in a crawl space

The City of Southlake page on protecting pipes during winter weather advises insulating exposed pipes in garages, attics, crawl spaces and exterior walls, and knowing where your main shutoff valve is. A pipe that bursts under the house can run for days before anyone sees it. If your crawl space holds supply lines, a quick look from the hatch before cold weather is cheap protection.

Our page on frozen pipe damage covers what to do after a thaw, and the burst pipe page covers pressurized line failures inside the structure.

Mold timing and insurance for water under the house

The Texas Department of Insurance says mold can start growing within a day after a leak. Wood, insulation and dark damp corners give it a head start, so quick extraction and drying matter. If you see visible growth, our mold remediation service handles that separately, and Texas licensing rules apply to testing and remediation.

Most Texas policies cover sudden and accidental water but not slow seepage or the mold it causes. Check your policy, ask your insurer what applies to your situation, and keep photos, receipts and damaged items for the adjuster. Ask any company whether its crews work to the published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard.

Mistakes to avoid under the floor

We reach nearby towns too, including Colleyville and Roanoke, through one dispatch line.

  • Climbing into water without checking for wiring or gas lines first.
  • Running a dehumidifier upstairs and assuming the problem is handled.
  • Closing the vents or hatch to hold in heat while the space is still wet.
  • Pulling out wet insulation without photographing it first.
  • Skipping the plumber when a pipe was the source.
