Basement Water Damage Cleanup in Southlake, TX

Water settles at the lowest point of a house, so a basement problem needs pumping, careful removal of wet finishes and steady drying.

While you wait, stay off wet stairs, keep away from outlets and the panel, and move anything you can reach to higher ground. If water is rising, do not walk into it, because it may be energized.

A basement water loss is any case where water enters or collects in a below-grade level, whether from groundwater, a failed pipe, a window well or an appliance. Not every Southlake home has a basement or finished lower level, so this page applies only if yours does. If it does, the same water can soak drywall, carpet and framing before you finish pumping.

Water in your basement right now

Work out the source first. A pipe or water heater calls for the main valve to be closed. Water that seeps through walls or floor slab after rain points to groundwater, and water pouring into a window well or stairwell points to surface drainage. A sump pump that has stopped is another common culprit.

Once the source is known or at least isolated, we send pumps and extraction units to remove standing water. Taking it out in stages matters because pulling water too fast from a flooded lower level can stress the walls. Crews decide pump rates on site.

Safety steps for the first hour below grade

Basements hold the panel, the water heater, the furnace and the washer, which puts water close to power and gas.

  • Do not enter standing water if outlets, the panel or appliances may be submerged.
  • If you smell gas or see sparks, leave and call 911 or the utility before calling us.
  • Shut off the main water valve if a pipe or fixture is feeding the water.
  • Keep stairs clear and stay out of any water that may be sewage.
  • Call Southlake Public Works at (817) 748-8082 for a city water or sewer concern.

Where basement water comes from and what it tells you

The pattern of the wet area usually points to the cause. A wet line along one wall suggests groundwater pushing through, while a ring around a drain suggests a backup. Different sources need different handling, and that includes who you call besides us.

Finished or unfinished: what changes in the cleanup

An unfinished basement with bare concrete and block is mostly an extraction and drying job. A finished one has carpet, pad, drywall, insulation, trim and sometimes cabinets, and each of those holds water differently.

Wet drywall that has soaked up past the wicking line is cut off in a flood cut, usually a couple of feet above the highest wet mark, so the framing can air out. Carpet and pad are checked and often removed. Crews tag items for your adjuster before they go in the dumpster.

How crews dry a lower level

After extraction, moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras map the wet areas in walls, floors and framing. Air movers circulate air across wet surfaces and LGR dehumidifiers pull the moisture out of the room, which matters in a space with little natural airflow.

Below-grade rooms dry more slowly than upper floors, so expect monitoring visits and adjustments over several days. HEPA air scrubbers may run when materials are opened. Our structural drying page explains the logging process, and the published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard is the reference to ask any company about.

What changes the cost of basement cleanup

Basement jobs scale with how much finish is down there and how long the water sat. These factors set the scope.

  • Water category and whether sewage or ground water was involved.
  • Depth and area of the water and the number of rooms affected.
  • Finished materials such as drywall, carpet, insulation and cabinets.
  • Drying days, equipment count and monitoring visits.
  • Rebuild scope once the structure is dry.

Flood maps, flood policies and what insurance expects

The Texas Department of Insurance says home and renters policies cover leaks that happen inside the home, including broken pipes, but not flood damage. Flood damage needs a separate flood policy, and an insurer may deny a claim if you do not protect your property after a loss. Check your policy, ask your insurer which cause applies to you, and keep receipts.

The city's interactive map shows 100-year and 500-year flood zones under the Environmental tab, and the FEMA Flood Map Service Center offers the federal view. Check both for your address. For storm and creek flooding, our flood damage restoration page covers the larger picture.

Mold timing in a damp lower level

The Texas Department of Insurance says mold can start growing within a day after a leak. A basement stays cool and damp, so wet drywall and carpet do not get a second chance. Most policies also exclude gradual seepage and the mold it causes, so fast action helps with the claim as well.

If you see visible growth, our mold remediation page explains how that work is handled. Texas licensing rules apply to testing and remediation, so ask which rules cover your job.

Mistakes to avoid after basement water

When the structure is dry, water damage repair covers rebuilding. We serve nearby towns such as Grapevine and Keller with the same line.

  • Wading in before power and gas are checked.
  • Leaving wet carpet and boxed items on the floor for days.
  • Running a household vacuum on standing water.
  • Painting or sealing walls before they are measured dry.
  • Assuming the problem is solved when the water is gone but the walls are still damp.
