Roof leak water damage in Southlake, TX is interior damage caused by rain that gets past shingles, flashing or a roof penetration and soaks the attic and the rooms below. In simple terms, the roof lets water in and the house holds it, so the damage keeps growing until the wet materials are dried or removed.

A roofer fixes the opening. We handle everything the water touched on the inside.

Water coming through the ceiling right now

The first goal is to limit what the water can reach. Put a bucket or a large pot under each drip and lay towels around it. If the ceiling drywall holds a pool of water, a bulge in the paint usually marks the spot.

Never climb onto a wet roof to look for the hole. Wet shingles are slick, and a fall costs far more than any leak. Photograph the stains from inside instead, and let a roofer inspect the roof from a ladder or a safe vantage point.

What the stain on your ceiling is telling you

Water from a roof rarely falls straight down. It runs along rafters and decking before it finds a low spot, so the stain you see can sit several feet from the actual opening. Use the table below to read what you are seeing.

Sagging or soft ceiling boardThe drywall has absorbed enough water to lose its strengthStay out from under it and call right away
Damp insulation or a musty smell in the atticThe leak has been running for a whileDo not walk on the insulation, and call for moisture mapping

First-hour safety steps before anyone calls a roofer

Water and ceiling lights are a bad mix. If water is dripping from a fixture, a ceiling fan or an outlet, do not touch it. Turn off the breaker for that room only if you can reach the panel without standing in water. If you cannot, leave the room and call 911 for any sign of sparks, burning smells or a buzzing sound.

A heavy, sagging ceiling can drop without warning, and wet drywall is heavier than it looks. Keep children and pets out of that room and move anything breakable aside. Do not run a ladder up to a wet roof, and do not go into the attic if the floor boards or insulation are soaked.

  • Shut off power to the wet room at the breaker only if the panel is dry and easy to reach.
  • Move rugs, electronics and furniture out from under the leak.
  • Take photos and video of every stain and drip before you clean anything.
  • Call a roofer for the opening and call us for the interior water.
  • Call 911 for sparks, smoke or a ceiling that is visibly failing.

How we dry a house after the roof has let water in

Roof water soaks from the top down, so we work the same way. A crew uses a moisture meter and a thermal imaging camera to trace the wet path through the ceiling drywall, the attic insulation and the framing. The camera shows cool, wet areas that the eye cannot see.

Wet insulation is bagged and removed because it holds water like a sponge. Saturated ceiling board is cut out past the stain so air can reach the framing, while board that is only damp stays and gets dried with air movers.

LGR dehumidifiers then pull moisture out of the air while air movers push dry air across the surfaces. Where attic framing is wet, the crew sets equipment to dry it without tearing out sound wood. Readings are checked on return visits until the materials match a dry reference.

We work to the published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration, and you should ask any company whether its crews work to it. For the stage that follows drying, see our water damage repair page, where the drywall, texture and paint get rebuilt.

People often ask who should come out first. The honest answer is both, close together. A roofer stops the opening, and we dry the inside so the damage does not spread while the roof waits for materials.

Temporary repairs such as a tarp are worth doing quickly. The Texas Department of Insurance tells homeowners to cover holes to keep rain out, save every receipt, and get more than one bid before signing for permanent work.

Roof permits, replacement cost and what your policy may pay

The City of Southlake permit guide lists replacing roofing materials or structural members as work that needs a permit. Your roofer should know this, but ask them who pulls it, and call Building Inspections at (817) 748-8236 if you are unsure. Check your homeowners association covenants too.

On the insurance side, the Texas Department of Insurance explains that replacement cost coverage can pay to repair a roof at current prices, while actual cash value pays less. Some policies pay less for an older or worn roof, and wind and hail deductibles can differ from your other deductible. Ask your agent which applies to you.

Insurers also do not pay for a new roof just because the old one is worn out. That is why photos of the damage, taken before anyone touches it, matter so much. Check your policy and ask your insurer what it requires.

Mistakes that make roof leaks worse

The most common mistake is waiting for the stain to dry on its own. A ceiling can look fine on the surface while the insulation above it stays wet for days. Mold can start growing within a day after water gets in, according to the Texas Department of Insurance.

Painting over a stain is the second mistake, since it hides what your adjuster wants to see. The third is paying a roofer in full before the work is finished.

Do not skip maintenance after the repair either. The state insurance department recommends checking for missing or damaged shingles after storms, keeping gutters clear, and trimming limbs that hang over the roof.
