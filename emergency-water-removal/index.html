Emergency Water Extraction in Southlake, TX

Pumps and extraction units get standing water out of your home so it stops soaking in.

☎ Call (817) 623-9267

Standing water in your house cannot sit, and our dispatch line at (817) 623-9267 runs 24/7 for emergency water extraction in Southlake, TX. While you wait, shut off the main water valve if a pipe is the source, stay out of water that touches outlets or appliances, and move anything valuable above the waterline. Do not use household vacuums on water, and leave the electrical panel alone if it is wet.

Emergency water extraction, often called water removal, is pulling standing water out of a building with submersible pumps and extraction units before it soaks into floors and walls. It comes first because water you can remove as a liquid is water you do not have to dry out of the framing later. Drying comes next, under water mitigation.

Standing water is in your home and you need it out

Water that sits does damage in layers. First it soaks carpet and pad, then baseboards and the bottom of the drywall, then subfloor. Pulling it out as liquid is the fastest way to limit all of that.

Crews pick the equipment by depth and surface. Deep water goes out through submersible pumps. Carpet gets weighted extraction wands, and tile or hard flooring gets wet vacuums and squeegees.

Speed matters because every inch of depth is more weight on the floor and more water reaching trim, cabinets, and walls. Crews also lift furniture legs onto blocks and pull rugs out of the water early. Baseboards are checked with a meter before extraction ends, since water often hides behind them.

Burst pipe water damage

Shut off the main water valve first. It is usually where the line enters the house, in a garage or utility room, or at the meter. Then open the lowest faucet to drain the lines. If the main will not turn, call your water utility or a plumber.

Freeze-and-thaw winters can burst pipes, and the February 2021 winter storm tested North Texas plumbing. Many burst-pipe calls start small. A braided supply line or stop valve under a sink can crack. A washer hose can split. A water heater tank can leak at the base.

For a water heater, shut the cold supply and switch off its power or gas only if you can reach the controls safely. After the source is closed, we extract the water and then dry the structure. A plumber repairs the pipe itself, because pipe repair is plumbing work and not what we do. What to expect: dispatch asks where the water is, a crew confirms it is off, then pumps go in and moisture mapping begins.

Electrical and sewage risk with standing water

Water can energize outlets, extension cords, and appliances. If you see sparks, hear buzzing, or smell gas, leave the house and call 911 or the utility before you call us. Do not enter a flooded room to reach the panel.

If the water looks gray, brown, or smells like sewage, treat it as contaminated. Keep children and pets away and say so on the call. If the trouble may be the city's water or sewer line, Southlake Public Works takes after-hours water and sewer emergencies at (817) 748-8082.

Crawl space with standing water

Water in a crawl space is easy to ignore and expensive to leave. It can come from a failed supply line, a drain leak, or runoff after heavy rain. Crews first work out which one it is, since pumping a plumbing leak that is still running only buys a few minutes.

Hoses carry the water out and away from the foundation. After that, the space needs drying and a check of insulation, the vapor barrier, and any wood in contact with moisture. Extraction is not waterproofing, so if water keeps returning, the fix is drainage or grading work. Homeowners and HOAs maintain their own property drainage, and city crews cannot use public resources to clean private property.

After the water is out: drying comes next

A room can look dry after extraction and still hold water in the subfloor, wall base, and insulation. Moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras find it, and air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry it. Skipping that step is how musty odors and warped boards show up weeks later.

If soaked materials need to come out, water damage cleanup handles the debris and sanitizing. If the water came from a storm or rising water outside, read about flood damage restoration and check the city's flood zone map and the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for your address.

Directions, nearby towns, and what to tell dispatch

Give us your street address plus the nearest main road, such as Southlake Boulevard or State Highway 114, and tell us about gates or locked entries. Crews need a place to park the extraction unit close to the door.

We also work calls in nearby towns such as Colleyville and the same steps apply. Say where the water is coming from, how deep it is, and which floor it is on.

Extraction is only the first stage of the published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for professional water damage restoration, which treats removal, drying and monitoring as one connected process. Ask any company you call whether its crews work to S500 and how they will confirm the structure is dry.
