Appliance Leak Water Damage Cleanup in Southlake, TX

A failed washer hose, dishwasher seal or ice-maker line can soak a floor before anyone notices, and we dry what it reached.

☎ Call (817) 623-9267

Appliance leak water damage cleanup in Southlake, TX means removing the water a washer, dishwasher, refrigerator or similar machine released and drying the floor, walls and cabinets it reached. The water usually comes from a supply hose, a drain hose, a door gasket or a small plastic fitting rather than from the machine's body.

Appliances sit in tight spots on purpose. A washer is pushed into a closet or alcove, a dishwasher is boxed in by cabinets, and a refrigerator stands against a wall, so a leak can run for days behind them while the room looks perfectly normal.

If water is on the floor right now, close the valve behind the machine, unplug it only if you can reach the cord on dry ground, and keep walking through the wet area to a minimum.

Sudden bursts and slow drips act very differently

A braided washer hose that lets go is a sudden event. Household pressure pushes a large volume across the laundry room in minutes and through any door gap into the hallway. The cleanup is mostly extraction, then drying of baseboards, drywall edges and the floor underneath.

A slow drip is quieter and often costs more. A dishwasher drain connection that weeps a little every cycle can wet the cabinet floor, the subfloor and the back of the neighboring cabinet for weeks. By the time a soft spot or a musty smell shows up, the damage is deeper than the surface suggests.

Appliance leaks leave clues that are easy to dismiss. This table matches the sign you see to the likely source and to what we would check with a moisture meter.

None of these signs prove a cause on their own. A plumber or appliance technician confirms the source, and our readings tell us how far the water traveled.

First-hour safety steps when a machine is leaking

Start with the closest shut-off. Most washers have two small valves on the wall behind them, and dishwashers and ice makers usually have a stop valve under the sink or at the line. If you cannot reach any of them, use the main water shut-off for the house.

Think about electricity before you step into standing water. If the water reaches outlets, a power strip or the base of the machine, do not touch it. Turn off the breaker only if the panel is dry and easy to reach. Otherwise leave the area and call a licensed electrician or 911 if anyone could be at risk.

If water is coming from the street side or the city's lines, the city's Public Works team takes after-hours water emergencies at (817) 748-8082. For a leak inside the home, a licensed plumber handles the fix, and we handle the water.

  • Close the valve behind or under the appliance, or the main water shut-off.
  • Keep children and pets away from the wet floor and the machine.
  • Pull rugs and small furniture off the wet area and prop up cabinet contents.
  • Take photos and short video of the water, the machine and the damage before moving much.
  • Do not run a household vacuum on standing water.

How we dry the floor, cabinet and wall behind a machine

Crews begin by pulling the appliance out of its slot if it can be moved safely. That exposes the wall, the floor and the cabinet side that were hiding the water. We then use an extraction unit to pull standing water from hard floors, and carpet gets extracted along with its pad.

Next comes mapping. A moisture meter and a thermal imaging camera show where water stopped, and we mark the edges. Toe kicks come off cabinets, and small holes may be drilled in the wall bottom so air can move through the cavity.

Air movers push dry air across the surfaces while LGR dehumidifiers pull moisture out of the room. Readings are checked on return visits until they match the dry reference points. We follow the published ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard as a reference, and you should ask any company whether its crews work to it.

What changes the cost of an appliance leak

Every appliance leak is a different size. These are the factors that move the scope of work, listed without dollar amounts because no honest number exists until someone reads the moisture in your walls and floor.

  • How long the leak ran before it was found.
  • Water category, which depends on whether the source was a clean supply line or a drain line.
  • Floor type, since tile, vinyl, hardwood and carpet all dry in different ways.
  • How many rooms the water reached, including the one below an upstairs laundry.
  • Whether cabinets, drywall or insulation must come out.

Insurance, a broken hose, and your paperwork

The Texas Department of Insurance says most homeowner and renter policies cover sudden and accidental water damage, and it lists a broken washer hose among the examples. The same guidance says gradual leaks and seepage are usually not covered. Check your policy for how it words that difference, and ask your insurer before assuming either way.

The same state guidance tells policyholders to photograph damage before repairs, keep damaged items until the adjuster has seen them, and save receipts. Keep the old hose or fitting in a bag if you can, because it shows how the failure happened.

We document moisture readings and affected areas in plain terms so you have something to hand your adjuster. We do not decide coverage and we never promise that a claim will be approved.

The City of Southlake permit guide says replacing fixtures on existing plumbing does not need a permit. That list is not all-inclusive, so call Building Inspections at (817) 748-8236 if the work goes beyond swapping a part, such as rerouting lines behind the wall.

Mistakes that make an appliance leak worse

  • Pushing the machine back into place before the floor behind it is dry.
  • Laying a towel over a wet cabinet floor and calling it done.
  • Reusing a rubber hose that already swelled or cracked.
  • Leaving a washer running while away from home.
  • Waiting to see whether a musty smell goes away on its own.
