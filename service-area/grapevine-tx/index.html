Grapevine Lake and what flood control does not cover

Grapevine Lake is a federal reservoir run by the US Army Corps of Engineers. Construction began in January 1948 and finished in June 1952, and the lake was authorized for flood control and navigation. It spans Denton County and Tarrant County and belongs to a coordinated floodway system along the Trinity River.

High water has still reached the lake's edges. In June 2015 flooding closed lake parks and boat ramps and put roads to sports fields under water, and Tarrant County received a disaster declaration on June 3, 2015. Reporting at the time said the damage fell mostly on public property and homes were largely spared. A less severe flood in 2007 took about 100 days to recede, according to a local news report.

The practical point for a homeowner is narrow. A reservoir manages river flow, and it does nothing about a split supply line inside your wall. Home policies do not cover floods, so rising surface water needs a separate flood policy, and you can check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for your address before you ever need it.

What water damage looks like inside Grapevine homes

Most calls start small and quiet. A stain spreads on a ceiling because an upstairs toilet seal let go. Hardwood begins to cup because an AC condensate line backed up, or a washer supply valve fails while nobody is home.

  • Ceiling stain below a bathroom: water is often still moving through the cavity, so it needs to be opened and dried before the drywall sags.
  • Cupped or buckled hardwood: moisture meters map how far the wet reaches under the boards.
  • Washer or sink supply failure: expect water inside the bottom of walls and under cabinets, not just on the floor.
  • Roof leak into the attic: wet insulation holds water against the ceiling below it.

Thermal imaging cameras help find wet areas behind finished surfaces, and moisture meters confirm what the camera suggests. Drying then runs on air movers and LGR dehumidifiers until the readings come back to normal.

If water is still spreading, start here

A toilet stop or washer valve closes in seconds. If you cannot find the right one, close the main water valve for the whole house.

Switch off breakers for wet rooms only if you can reach the panel without stepping in water. Otherwise stay back and call the utility.

Lift the valuables

Get electronics, documents, photos and shoes onto counters and beds. Pull rugs off wet hardwood so the finish does not stain.

Stay out of dirty water

Water from a toilet, drain or outdoors can carry contamination. Keep children and pets out of it.

Take photos or video of each wet room and keep damaged items for the adjuster. Texas Department of Insurance guidance asks for this.
