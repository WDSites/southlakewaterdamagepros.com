Water Damage Restoration in Stone Lakes, Southlake, TX

Stone Lakes homes are now decades old, which makes the age of your supply lines, water heater and roof worth knowing before a leak starts.

Water damage restoration in Stone Lakes means tracing the leak, removing the water and drying every wet layer before it spreads. Southlake Water Damage Pros takes calls from Stone Lakes homeowners 24/7 and sends licensed, insured restoration professionals.

Stone Lakes is an established Southlake subdivision with its own association and shared amenities. That makes the line between your repair and the association's repair worth drawing early. Our main Southlake page explains how the 24/7 line works for the whole service area.

Where Stone Lakes is and what it includes

A Community Impact neighborhood profile describes Stone Lakes as one of the neighborhoods south of FM 1709 in Southlake, within a mile of Bicentennial Park. The profile says the subdivision has more than 300 homes and was built out in 1997.

It lists a park, playground, clubhouse, bike path, lake, tennis courts and pool as amenities. The profile dates from 2013 and 2014, so treat the numbers as a guide and confirm details with your association.

What a 1997 build-out means for leak planning

Homes finished around 1997 are now many years old. That does not mean they leak more. It is a reason to ask when the water heater, supply lines, washer hoses and roof were last replaced, because those are the parts that fail suddenly.

Keep a short record of those replacements. If a loss happens, the record helps you and your insurer see the cause. Our water heater leak cleanup page covers what to do when a tank fails.

Your house, your association's amenities

A clubhouse, pool or lake belongs to the association, not to you. A leak in those areas is the association's call. A leak in your house is yours. The City of Southlake says homeowners and associations maintain their own property, and city crews cannot use public resources on private property.

If water from a common area is reaching your lot, document it and tell the association. If it comes from a city street or main, call Southlake Public Works at (817) 748-8082.

Safety steps before the crew arrives

Water and electricity do not mix, so keep out of any room where cords or outlets sit in standing water. If the main valve is dry and within reach, close it. A breaker panel is not worth a shock, so leave it alone while the floor is wet.

Take photos, then lift valuables off wet floors. A gas smell, sparks or a sagging ceiling means leave and call 911. Once everyone is safe, call (817) 623-9267 and describe what you see. See emergency water extraction for what the first visit involves.

Insurance questions for older homes

The Texas Department of Insurance says sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, while gradual leaks are not. A slow drip that went unnoticed for months may be treated differently from a burst line.

Ask your insurer how it classifies your loss. Dated photos, moisture readings and a written scope help describe it, and none of them promises a result. A slow leak is a mold remediation concern too, so call early.

How drying proceeds after extraction

After the standing water is out, moisture meters locate wet drywall, baseboards and subfloor. Air movers and dehumidifiers run for days, and crews return to take readings. Our water damage cleanup and water damage repair pages cover the stages before and after.

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard. Ask any company whether its crews work to it.
