Water Damage Restoration in Carillon, Southlake, TX

Carillon mixes homes, lakes and commercial districts, so the first question after a leak is who owns the wall the water came through.

☎ Call (817) 623-9267

Water damage restoration in Carillon is the work of finding the source, removing standing water and drying walls, floors and ceilings before damage spreads. Southlake Water Damage Pros takes calls from Carillon homes and buildings at all hours and sends licensed, insured restoration professionals.

Carillon is a master-planned community, and that changes the first phone call. A leak in a detached home is your repair. A leak in a shared or commercial building may also involve a property manager.

What Carillon is and where it sits

A 2012 Southlake Style report on the developer's announcement describes Carillon as a 285-acre mixed-use community at the northeast corner of State Highway 114 and White's Chapel Road. It sits northwest of Southlake Town Center and lies in Carroll ISD.

The same report names a Village District of garden homes with enclosed-front courtyards, a Chateaux District of estate-style homes near community lakes and ponds, and Plaza and Corporate districts for retail and office use. It also cites 68 acres of parks and green space. These details come from 2012, so confirm what is built on your street.

Homes, courtyards and shared buildings

In a detached or courtyard home the owner decides on repairs. In an office or retail space the lease and the property manager decide who calls whom. Tell dispatch which situation you are in and which entrance or unit the crew should use.

Water from an upstairs unit or a rooftop can reach a neighbor below. Notify the property manager and the tenant below before anyone moves wet materials. Our commercial water damage page explains what a crew can handle in a commercial space.

Water does not need a storm

In December 2011 a broken fire-protection water line flooded the first floor of Southlake Town Hall with about 100,000 gallons, according to NBC DFW. The report said it was not rainwater. It shows that a failed pipe can do the damage in any building.

In homes the same lesson applies to supply lines, water heaters and AC drain lines. We do not say these fail more often in Carillon. We say the response is the same: stop the source, then dry the structure. Our burst pipe water damage page covers that first stage.

Lakes, ponds and what counts as a flood

Because the developer's materials mention lakes and ponds, some owners ask whether high water is a concern. We found no documented flood history for Carillon and do not claim one. If water rises from outside, that is a flood question, not a plumbing one.

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. Use the City of Southlake's interactive map, under the Environmental base layers, and FEMA's Flood Map Service Center to check your address. Standard homeowners insurance does not cover floods, so read your policy.

Safety steps for the first hour

If lights, cords or appliances touch standing water, stay out of the room. Close the main valve when you can reach it dry. Household fans and an ordinary vacuum are not enough for sewage or contaminated water, so do not use them there.

Photograph the damage when it is safe, and protect dry belongings. Call 911 for sparks, a gas smell or a failing ceiling. For city water or sewer lines call Southlake Public Works at (817) 748-8082. Then call (817) 623-9267.

Insurance records for Carillon owners and tenants

The Texas Department of Insurance says sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, while gradual leaks and floods usually are not. A tenant should also check the lease and the property owner's policy. Ask your insurer which one applies.

Dated photos, moisture readings and a written scope help describe the loss. They do not guarantee a payout. Keep damaged materials until the adjuster has seen them.

Extraction and drying in practice

Pumps or extraction wands remove standing water. Moisture meters and thermal cameras show where water traveled, and air movers and dehumidifiers dry the rest. Our emergency water extraction page describes the first step in detail.

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published water damage restoration standard. Ask any company whether its crews work to it.
