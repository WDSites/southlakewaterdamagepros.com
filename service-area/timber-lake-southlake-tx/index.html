Pipe water or surface water?

Water from a split supply line, a failed water heater or an AC drain line is a plumbing loss. Water that enters at a door or foundation after heavy rain is surface water. The two are handled and insured differently, so tell dispatch what you saw.

The Texas Department of Insurance says home policies do not cover floods, which need a separate policy. Check your policy before you assume either way. Our flood damage restoration page covers surface and storm water.

Who maintains drainage around your lot

The City of Southlake says its engineers assess public drainage and its Streets and Drainage Team clears paths for first responders after storms. It also says homeowners and associations are responsible for their own property.

If a greenbelt or common drain is involved, ask your association. If a city street or main is involved, call Southlake Public Works at (817) 748-8082. Photograph where the water entered.

Large floor plans and how crews plan the work

The profile's average size suggests many Timber Lake homes are large. A bigger footprint can mean more rooms to check and more equipment to place, even when the leak was small. Moisture mapping shows how far water traveled before anything is opened.

Safety steps in the first hour

Treat any standing water near a plug, cord or appliance as live until proven otherwise. Close the main water valve only if you can do it from dry ground. Keep electrical tools out of the water, and call water mitigation crews before you start pulling up flooring.

Photograph the damage once everyone is safe. A gas smell, sparks or a sagging ceiling means call 911 and leave. After that, call (817) 623-9267 and tell dispatch where the water entered.

Documenting a loss for your insurer

Photos, moisture readings and a written scope can describe the loss. They do not guarantee approval. Keep damaged items until the adjuster has seen them.

Ask your insurer how it treats your cause of loss, and check your policy for flood coverage if surface water is involved. ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published water damage restoration standard, so ask any company whether its crews work to it.
