Haul out what cannot be saved, sanitize what can, and get your rooms ready for drying and rebuilding.

Water damage cleanup is the removal of standing water, wet debris, and materials that cannot be saved, followed by sanitizing and deodorizing the space. It begins once the source is stopped and usually fills the first 48 hours. Drying is covered under water mitigation, and rebuilding comes later.

The leak is stopped but the soaked mess is still in your house right now

A closed valve ends the flow, not the problem. Carpet pad is holding gallons, drywall is pulling water up the wall, and insulation is packed against framing. Each hour these stay wet, they get harder to save and the smell gets worse.

Cleanup crews work room by room. They pull standing water, remove what is past saving, bag and haul debris, then clean and sanitize every surface that stays. The goal is a bare, clean structure that drying equipment can work on.

Electrical and sewage safety during cleanup

Do not walk through water if outlets, cords, or appliances are sitting in it. If you see sparks or smell gas, leave and call 911 or the utility first, then call us.

Water that came up through a floor drain or toilet from the sewer is not a do-it-yourself job. Keep people and pets away and tell dispatch right away, since the crew will set up containment and protective gear before removing anything.

Water categories 1, 2, and 3: what each one means for cleanup

Restoration professionals sort water into three categories under the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard. The category decides what gets saved and what goes in the dumpster. Time matters because clean water that sits for days can slip into a higher category.

CategoryTypical sourcePorous materialsCleanup approach
1Broken supply line, washer hose, sink overflow from clean supplyCarpet and pad can often be dried in place if caught earlyExtraction, drying, light cleaning
2Dishwasher or washer discharge, some toilet overflows, sump overflowPad usually removed, carpet cleaned or replacedExtract, remove pad, clean and sanitize
3Sewage backup, or water that sat long enough to degradePorous materials removed and discardedContainment, removal, disinfecting, HEPA air scrubbers

Rising water from storms is a separate case handled under flood damage restoration, since it is treated as heavily contaminated from the start.

What gets removed and what can be saved

No one wants to throw out a house worth of materials, and crews do not do it casually. Each item gets a moisture reading and a category check before it goes in the pile.

Soaked carpet pad

Pad acts like a sponge and is usually the first thing out. The carpet on top can sometimes be lifted, cleaned, and dried if the water was clean.

Drywall that is wicking

Water climbs the wall above the visible line. Crews check with a meter and cut out wet sections up to the highest reading.

Wet insulation

Batt insulation slumps and holds water against framing, so it generally comes out. Dense foam can sometimes stay.

Hardwood and subfloor

Caught early, they often dry in place. Cupping and gaps show up when water sat too long.

Furniture and contents

Solid wood pieces often clean up well. Upholstery touched by category 2 or 3 water usually does not.

Papers and electronics

Set them aside dry and do not power anything on. Keep damaged items until your adjuster has seen them.

Sanitizing and deodorizing after the debris is gone

Cleaning agents go on only after wet debris is out, because sanitizer cannot fix a saturated pad. Crews wipe and treat remaining surfaces, run HEPA air scrubbers to pull fine particles from the air, and use deodorizing products where odor lingers.

A smell that returns usually points to something still wet or something left behind. Deodorizing masks nothing when the source is removed, which is the reason removal comes first. If dark spotting appears after cleanup, that is a job for mold remediation rather than more sanitizer.

Content pack-out and what to set aside

Pack-out means moving belongings out of the affected rooms so crews can work and so the items do not soak up moisture or odor. Furniture, clothing, and décor get sorted into salvageable, cleanable, and discard piles.

Before the pile grows, tell your insurer and document it. The Texas Department of Insurance advises photos or video before repairs and keeping damaged items until the adjuster has seen them.

  • Photograph each room and each damaged item before it moves.
  • Keep a list of what you discard, with rough age and brand.
  • Hold back valuables and papers for separate drying or cleaning.
  • Save every receipt for temporary repairs and hauling.
