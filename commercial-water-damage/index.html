Commercial Water Damage Restoration in Southlake, TX

Water extraction, structural drying and cleanup for commercial space, with the paperwork trail your property manager and insurer will ask for.

☎ Call (817) 623-9267

Commercial water damage restoration in Southlake, TX is the removal of standing water and the controlled drying of a non-residential building so the space can be repaired and reopened. A leak in a commercial space rarely stays in one room. Water follows shared walls, ceiling plenums and floor slabs into neighboring suites, and the longer it runs the more of the building it touches.

Our dispatch line is open 24/7. While you wait for a crew, shut off the water supply to the affected area if you can reach the valve, keep people out of wet zones, and call your property manager or building owner so the right person knows.

This page covers what we can do on a commercial loss: extraction, drying, cleanup and coordination. It also covers what we leave to others, such as the plumber, the electrician and the roofer.

Water in your building right now

If water is coming down from a ceiling, rising from a floor drain or spreading across a lobby, the first minutes matter more than the first phone call. Find the nearest shut-off for the line that is leaking. In a building with several spaces, that valve may sit in a utility room or mechanical closet, and your property manager will know where.

Do not walk through wet areas near electrical panels, floor outlets or lit fixtures. If water is touching electrical equipment, leave the area and call 911 or the electric utility before you call us.

Then call (817) 623-9267. Dispatch is staffed around the clock and same-day response is offered when crews are available, so an after-hours or weekend leak is treated like any other.

What you see and what it usually means

Commercial buildings hide water well. Suspended ceiling tiles, wall cavities and carpet over a slab all absorb moisture before anything looks wrong. This table matches common signs to the likely problem.

What you seeWhat it usually meansFirst step
Brown or sagging ceiling tilesWater above the ceiling from a supply line, drain line or roofKeep people out from under the tiles and shut off the source
Wet carpet near a wallA leak inside the wall or a supply line behind itShut off water to that area and call a plumber
Water bubbling from a floor drainA blocked drain or sewer line, which may be contaminatedStay out of the water and see the sewage cleanup page
Musty smell in a closed suiteMoisture trapped behind finishes or under flooringCheck with moisture meters before anything is opened
Peeling paint or stained baseboardSlow seepage, sometimes from a roof or exterior wallPhotograph it and find the entry point

Visible damage is usually the smaller part of the loss. A moisture meter and a thermal imaging camera show where the water has actually gone, which is why we scan beyond the stain before we price anything.

Safety steps for the first hour

Safety comes before salvage in a commercial building. More people use the space, more equipment is plugged in, and the shut-off may not be where you expect it.

  • Shut off the water to the leaking line, or ask your property manager to close the building supply.
  • Turn off power to wet areas at the breaker only if the panel is dry and you can reach it safely.
  • If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 or the gas utility before anyone else.
  • Call a licensed plumber to stop and repair a failed pipe. We dry and clean up but do not replace plumbing.
  • For a problem on the city side of the meter, call Public Works at (817) 748-8082 for water or sewer emergencies.
  • Keep customers and staff out of wet or sagging areas until the space is checked.

Photograph the damage before anything is moved. Your insurer and your property manager will both want a record of how the space looked when you found it.

How the crew works in a commercial space

We start with extraction. Truck-mounted or portable extraction units pull standing water from carpet, tile and slab, and a moisture map of floors and walls shows what is still wet. Dehumidification then works against the humid Texas air that would otherwise slow the drying.

Drying equipment goes where the readings say. Air movers push air across wet surfaces, LGR dehumidifiers pull moisture out of the room, and HEPA air scrubbers run when dust or debris is stirred up during demolition. Materials that cannot be saved are removed so the cavity behind them can dry.

We return to read the meters until the materials are dry, then remove the equipment. The same approach is described in our structural drying page, and the first stage is covered under emergency water extraction. We do not rent equipment.

Working with a property manager and neighbors

In a multi-suite building, a loss often involves more than one party. The property manager controls access to shared walls, ceilings and the roof, and a neighboring space may be where the water started. We work with the manager on access, shut-offs and the order of rooms to dry.

Address and access details help dispatch. Tell us the street, the suite or floor, whether the entrance sits near Southlake Boulevard, State Highway 114 or the Town Square area, and where a crew can unload equipment.

The city also supports commercial work. Southlake Building Inspections at 1400 Main Street, Suite 250, serves homeowners, builders and commercial projects, and its page says general contractors register with the city every year and trade contractors with a current state license must register too. Ask any contractor you hire for repairs whether they are registered, and call Building Inspections at (817) 748-8236 to ask whether your repair needs a permit.

What changes the cost of a commercial job

No two commercial losses are priced alike. We explain scope and price before work starts, and these are the factors that move it.

  • Water category: clean supply water, gray water or sewage-contaminated water each need a different plan.
  • Area affected: the number of rooms, floors and shared walls the water reached.
  • Materials involved: carpet tile, ceiling tile, drywall, insulation and flooring over slab dry and fail differently.
  • Drying days and equipment: the number of air movers and dehumidifiers and how many days they run.
  • Access and hours: working around business hours or a locked building changes the schedule.
  • Rebuild scope: repairs after drying are priced separately from mitigation.

Insurance often matters here. Check your policy, because commercial property policies vary, and ask your insurer whether sudden water damage, gradual leaks and sewer backup are treated differently. We do not promise approval of any claim. Want your real number? Call (817) 623-9267.

Mistakes that make a commercial loss worse

Waiting for the next business day is the most common mistake. Water keeps moving through porous material overnight, and mold can start growing in as little as a day after a leak according to the Texas Department of Insurance.

Throwing away damaged items before the adjuster has seen them is another. Keep damaged materials, photograph the loss before cleanup and save receipts for any temporary repairs.

Mixed-use communities such as Carillon combine homes with retail and office space. Our Carillon page explains how that changes the first call.

Standards, references and neighboring services
