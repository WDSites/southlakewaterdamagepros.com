Emergency Sewage Backup Cleanup in Southlake, TX

Contaminated water from a drain is not a do-it-yourself mop job, so call early and keep out of it.

☎ Call (817) 623-9267

If sewage is coming up from a toilet, tub or floor drain, our dispatch line is open 24/7 at (817) 623-9267. While you wait, stop using every sink, toilet and shower, keep children and pets out of the area and do not wade through it.

A sewage backup in Southlake, TX is waste water that reverses direction and flows back into the house through its lowest drains. It is the most contaminated category of water, usually called Category 3, and it needs different handling than a leaking pipe or rainwater.

The cleanup removes the water, discards what it soaked, and sanitizes what is left.

Sewage is coming up right now

Stop all water use at once. Every flush, shower or load of laundry sends more water into the line that is already full, and it will come out at the same low drain. Tell everyone in the house.

Keep people and pets out of the affected room and shut that door if you can. Do not try to clear the clog with a plunger or chemical drain cleaner, because that can push contaminated water further. Do not run a fan or vacuum to dry it.

Wear rubber boots and gloves if you must walk near it, and wash your hands afterward. Take photos from the doorway so you do not need to step in.

Safety steps before cleanup begins

Treat the water as contaminated, and treat the electrical system with care. If sewage has reached outlets, appliances or the breaker panel, do not touch them. Call 911 or your electric utility before you call us if you see sparks or smell gas.

Do not use a household vacuum on sewage water, since it can spread contamination through the exhaust. Do not eat or drink in the area. Keep the heating and cooling system off if water reached ductwork or the air handler.

  • Stop using every toilet, sink, tub and washing machine.
  • Turn off power to wet rooms only if the panel is dry and reachable.
  • Keep children, pets and visitors out of the affected area.
  • Wear boots, gloves and eye protection if you must enter.
  • Do not mix bleach with other cleaners, because the fumes are dangerous.

Whose pipe is it, the city's or yours

The cause decides who fixes it. The city's website says that for a sewer backup or slow drains, you should call its Wastewater Division first at (817) 748-8082. Staff clean and check the sewer main to find out whether a plumber is needed.

The city also says residents are responsible for cleaning and repairing the service line from the house to and including the tap. If part of that line lies under the road, the city repairs that section. Contact the Wastewater Division before calling a plumber so nobody pays for work on the wrong side of the line.

Public Works operations is at 1950 E. Continental Blvd., Southlake, TX 76092, and the same number reaches them. After the line is clear and repaired, we come in to handle the water and what it touched.

How we remove and sanitize a sewage loss

A crew arrives in protective gear and sets up containment barriers with plastic sheeting to keep contamination out of clean rooms. Standing water is removed with an extraction unit. A sump and transfer pump can move larger volumes to an approved discharge.

Porous materials that sewage has soaked are bagged and discarded. That includes carpet, pad, insulation and the lower part of wet drywall. Hard surfaces and framing are cleaned, treated with an antimicrobial and left to dry.

HEPA air scrubbers clean the air while air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry the space. Moisture readings confirm the structure is dry before the room is released for rebuild. We do not rent equipment, so you will not find us handing you a fan.

For the cleanup that follows, see our water damage cleanup page. For drying detail, structural drying explains how readings are tracked.

Why raw sewage is thrown out, not dried

Clean water can often be dried in place. Sewage cannot be treated the same way. The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency publishes guidance on cleaning up after contaminated water, and the practical lesson is that porous materials soaked in sewage are generally removed instead of saved.

Ask any company whether its crews follow the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration and the EPA guidance for contaminated water. Those references are published for the industry, and they are not a certification held by us.

What changes the cost of sewage cleanup

Because sewage is the most involved water category, the price depends on a few things we measure on site. We explain scope and price before work starts.

  • The area and number of rooms the water reached.
  • How much carpet, pad, insulation and drywall must be removed.
  • Containment, protective gear and disposal requirements.
  • Drying days and equipment such as air scrubbers and dehumidifiers.
  • Rebuild scope after sanitizing, priced separately.

Insurance depends on the cause. A sudden backup is sometimes covered, but many policies need a specific endorsement for sewer and drain backup. Check your policy and ask your insurer. Want your real number? Call (817) 623-9267.

Mistakes to avoid after a backup

The biggest mistake is cleaning it up like a spill. Mopping and wet vacuuming spread contamination, and a damp carpet left in place keeps the odor alive. Another is mixing bleach with ammonia-based cleaners, which creates dangerous fumes.

Do not leave it for later either. Mold can begin growing within a day after water gets in, according to the Texas Department of Insurance. Keep damaged items until the adjuster sees them, and photograph everything first.
