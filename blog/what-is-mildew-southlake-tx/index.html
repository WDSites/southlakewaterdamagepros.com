What Is Mildew? A Plain Guide for Southlake, TX

How mildew relates to mold, where it shows up at home, and what you can safely do about it.

☎ Call (817) 623-9267

By the Southlake Water Damage Pros team · Updated 2026

Mildew is a surface growth of mold, usually flat, powdery or downy, and often white, gray or yellowish. In Southlake, TX, it tends to appear where a bathroom, closet or window stays damp.

Mildew and mold are not two unrelated things, and our crews in Grapevine and Colleyville see both words used for the same sort of patch. People use mildew for light growth on a surface and mold for deeper or heavier growth, but both need moisture. This guide covers where mildew appears, what you can clean yourself, and when a water damage repair or removal pro is the better call.

Mildew and mold: how the words differ

In everyday talk, mildew means thin growth sitting on top of a surface, like the film on a shower curtain. Mold is the broader word and often describes growth that has taken hold in or under a material.

The distinction is informal. A single organism could be called either depending on how far along it is, so the label tells you little about what to do. The better question is how wet the material is and how long it has been that way.

What mildew looks and feels like

Mildew is usually flat. It can look like a dusty coating, a faint web or a gray patch that smudges when touched.

Colors vary, and the color alone does not identify it. It often comes with a damp, stale smell. If it wipes off and the surface underneath is solid, it is more likely to be surface growth. If the surface underneath is soft, stained through or warped, the moisture has gone deeper than a wipe can reach, and that changes the plan.

Where mildew shows up around the house

Look anywhere moisture lingers and air barely moves.

A few places give it away more often than others.

Showers and tubs

Grout lines, caulk, curtains and the corners where water pools.

Window sills

Condensation on cold glass drips down and soaks the frame and sill.

Closets

Packed shelves and shut doors trap damp air, especially on exterior walls.

Under sinks

A slow drip at a supply line can keep the cabinet floor wet.

Laundry rooms

Damp towels, wet floors and an unvented dryer add moisture.

Garage and storage areas

Cardboard boxes and cloth items soak up humidity.

Cleaning light mildew safely

For a small patch on a hard surface like tile, glass or sealed fixtures, gentle cleaning is reasonable. Use a detergent and water solution and a soft brush, wear gloves, and ventilate the room.

Never mix cleaning products, especially bleach with ammonia. Dry the surface afterward with a towel, and leave the fan running for a while. Toss porous items like a mildewed bath mat or shower curtain liner if cleaning does not restore them. The point is not just the stain. It is removing the damp that fed it, so the same patch has less reason to return.

  • Run the bathroom exhaust fan during and after showers.
  • Wipe down wet shower walls and window sills.
  • Leave closet doors slightly open and give shelves room.
  • Fix drips and replace failed caulk.
  • Keep indoor humidity low with air conditioning and a dehumidifier.

The 25 square foot threshold, stated carefully

Texas uses 25 contiguous square feet of visible mold as a key threshold for its licensing rules. That is about a five-by-five patch. Owners may do some work on their own property under exemptions, so rules for your situation can differ.

Ask TDLR or the contractor which rules apply to your job. For larger areas, a licensed assessor and remediator are involved, and our mold remediation page describes how that works.

Moisture is the thing to fix

Cleaning removes what you can see. It does not dry the wall. Moisture meters can show whether framing or drywall is still wet, and thermal imaging cameras help trace hidden leaks.

We are honest about the limits. Restoration dries and repairs, but it is not waterproofing, and growth can return anywhere moisture does. For questions about health, a physician is the right person to ask, not us.

Professional mold remediation follows the published ANSI/IICRC S520 standard, which is worth asking about if a cleanup grows beyond a small surface. Ask any company whether its crews work to S520 before you hire it.

When to call

Call when mildew keeps returning, when a wall feels damp or soft, when you see staining after a leak, or when the affected area is larger than you can handle. Photograph it, note when you first saw it, and write down where the nearest water source is.

Southlake homeowners and the state rules on mold

The Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation licenses mold assessors and remediators and sets the 25 contiguous square feet threshold that shapes its rules. Sampling to find out whether mold is present requires a licensed assessor, and the samples go to a licensed lab, so a stain on your wall cannot be named by a neighbor or by color.

The Texas Department of Insurance says mold can begin growing within a day after a leak, and that most policies do not cover gradual leaks or the mold they cause. If you suspect a hidden leak, report it promptly, take photos before cleaning, and ask your insurer what applies.
