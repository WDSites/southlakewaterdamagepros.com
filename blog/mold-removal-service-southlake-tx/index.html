Mold Removal Service in Southlake, TX: How to Vet One

Questions to ask, documents to expect and warning signs to walk away from.

☎ Call (817) 623-9267

By the Southlake Water Damage Pros team · Updated 2026

A mold removal service is a company licensed to remove mold-damaged material and clean affected areas under a written plan. In Southlake, TX, hiring the wrong one can mean paying twice, so a few pointed questions up front are worth the time.

Texas splits the work between licensed assessors who write the plan and licensed remediators who carry it out. This guide shows what to ask, what a written scope should contain, and which red flags mean you should keep looking. Our own mold remediation page explains how that process runs from our side.

Understand the two licenses before you call anyone

The Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation licenses mold assessors (technician, consultant and company) and mold remediators (contractor and company). Assessors sample and write protocols. Remediators do the removal.

Ask each company which license it holds and look it up on the state's license search. Keep the license record with your paperwork, since an insurer or lender may ask for it later. A company that says it does everything should be able to explain how it keeps assessment and removal properly separated on your job.

Questions to ask on the first call

Write the answers down. A reputable company will not mind.

  • Are you a TDLR-licensed mold remediation contractor, and can I verify the license?
  • Will a licensed consultant write the protocol, and is that someone independent from you?
  • Who does the clearance testing after the work?
  • Do your crews work to ANSI/IICRC S520, the published standard for mold remediation?
  • How will you contain the work area, and how will you filter the air?
  • What is the moisture source, and who is fixing it?

What a written scope should list

A real scope reads like a plan, not a one-line quote. It names rooms, materials and methods so you can compare bids honestly.

Expect it to say which materials come out and which are cleaned, how containment is set up, what equipment runs, and how waste leaves the home. It should also say what happens to drying afterward and what the clearance step requires. Scope and price should be explained before work starts.

  • Areas and approximate sizes involved.
  • Containment barriers and negative air with HEPA air scrubbers.
  • Removal of affected porous materials, and cleaning of what stays.
  • Moisture readings before and after, taken with a moisture meter.
  • Independent clearance requirements and who arranges them.
  • Rebuild, which is often a separate scope.

Why independent clearance matters

Clearance is the check that the work achieved its goal. In Texas, a consultant who did not write the protocol cannot declare clearance, which keeps the same party from both setting the target and grading itself.

Insurers and lenders often ask for it. A certificate of removal and proof the cause was fixed are what the insurer will want on a mold claim, so ask upfront what paperwork you will receive.

Red flags worth walking away from

A few signals appear again and again. No containment is the biggest one, since work done without barriers can spread debris through the house.

Others include no plan for the moisture source, pressure to sign immediately, a refusal to name a license, a promise that growth will never return, or a single company acting as assessor, remediator and clearance tester on the same job. We never promise results no one can control, and neither should anyone you hire.

Where water restoration fits in

Many mold jobs start as water jobs. If wet materials are still wet, drying has to happen alongside removal or the problem returns, and that is where water damage cleanup work comes in.

If you are comparing a local company against one from farther away, ask each of them the same list and hold them to the same written answers. A company that is vague on the phone rarely becomes specific on paper.

When to call

Call when you can see growth you suspect covers a large area, when a lender or insurer has asked for removal, or when a leak has kept a wall wet for several days. If you are unsure which professional to start with, ask for guidance before signing anything, and compare at least two written scopes.

What Southlake residents can verify with the state before signing

The Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation lists licensed mold professionals, and a Southlake homeowner can check a name before agreeing to anything. Minimum work practices apply to licensed remediation, and the 25 contiguous square feet threshold shapes which rules apply, so ask TDLR or the contractor how they apply to your job.

The Texas Department of Insurance says to use a state-licensed mold professional on a mold claim, and to expect the insurer to want proof that the mold was removed and the cause fixed. Most policies do not include mold cleanup and testing after a damaged item is removed, though some insurers let you add that coverage, so ask yours.
