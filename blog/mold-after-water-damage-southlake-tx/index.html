Mold After Water Damage in Southlake, TX

What happens hour by hour once a building gets wet, and what slows it down.

☎ Call (817) 623-9267

By the Southlake Water Damage Pros team · Updated 2026

Mold after water damage is the growth that follows when building materials stay wet long enough for spores already in the air to settle and feed. The Texas Department of Insurance notes that mold can start growing within a day after a leak, which is why Southlake, TX, homeowners hear so much about the first 24 to 48 hours.

This timeline walks through what changes as water sits, what you can do right away, and where a water mitigation crew fits in. It is a general guide, not an assessment of any particular house.

Hour zero: the water arrives

A burst pipe, a failed washer hose or a roof leak soaks drywall, insulation, flooring and framing within minutes. Water wicks upward in drywall and spreads sideways under flooring, often far past the stain you see.

The most useful thing to do now is stop the source. Shut off the main water valve, lift valuables, and keep people and pets out of any water that may be contaminated. If the panel is wet or you smell gas, leave and call the utility or 911 before anyone else.

The first day: drying decides what happens next

In the first day, the goal is removing standing water and starting air movement. Extraction units pull water from carpet and floors, and air movers push dry air across wet surfaces.

Mold needs moisture, so the speed of drying matters more than anything sprayed on a wall. Pulling up soaked carpet pad early often keeps the subfloor from staying wet underneath. Cleaning products do not replace drying. A surface that looks clean but stays wet behind the paint is still a place for growth to begin.

Hours 24 to 48: the window that matters

This is the stretch most restoration pros point to. Materials that are still wet after a day or two begin to favor growth, particularly in warm, closed-up rooms.

LGR dehumidifiers pull moisture out of the air, and moisture meters confirm whether drywall and framing are actually returning to normal. Thermal imaging cameras help find wet spots behind walls without cutting them open. Our crews rely on readings, not guesses, to decide when drying is done.

Days three to five: visible changes can start

If wet material is left in place, you may notice spots, fuzzy texture or a persistent musty smell. These are signs of moisture trouble, though color or smell alone cannot tell you what is growing.

Porous materials such as insulation, ceiling tile and carpet padding are the hardest to save once they stay wet. Removal of those items is often part of mitigation, and it is done early because it is simpler than dealing with growth later.

What to do while you wait for help

Small steps protect your home and your insurance position.

  • Take photos and video of the damage before you move anything.
  • Keep damaged items until the adjuster has seen them.
  • Make temporary repairs, like tarping a roof, to limit further damage.
  • Remove soaked rugs and loose items so air can reach the floor.
  • Run the air conditioner to help pull moisture out of the air.
  • Keep receipts for anything you buy to reduce the damage.

Why drying and mold work are separate jobs

A drying crew restores the building to a dry condition. Mold work, if it turns out to be needed, is regulated in Texas and follows a written protocol. If a house stays wet long enough that growth appears, a licensed removal team enters the picture, with containment and clearance steps of its own.

Cleanup after a spill, such as the soaked carpet and pad, falls under water damage cleanup, which is a different scope than assessing or removing growth.

We take calls from Colleyville and Roanoke as well, and the clock works the same way in every town. Wet drywall in one house behaves like wet drywall in the next, so the speed of the first response is the part you can control. Keep the call short and the details ready: where the water came from, what it touched, and when it started.

ANSI/IICRC S520 is the published standard for professional mold remediation, and ANSI/IICRC S500 covers the water damage work that comes before it. Ask any company which of the two its crews follow for each stage of your job.

When to call

Call as soon as you know water has entered the house, even if it seems small. Waiting a day to see whether things dry on their own is the choice that most often lets damage spread.

Texas insurance and licensing facts that matter after a leak

The Texas Department of Insurance says most homeowner and renter policies cover sudden and accidental water damage, such as a burst pipe or a toilet overflow. Mold that grows on a covered damaged item is covered too. Gradual leaks and seepage are generally not covered, nor is the mold they cause, and floods need a separate flood policy.

For hidden water damage, policyholders usually need to report it within days after first seeing it, so check your policy promptly. For mold claims, the state expects a state-licensed mold professional, proof that the cause was fixed, and a certificate of removal. Hold off on permanent repairs until the insurer has inspected.
