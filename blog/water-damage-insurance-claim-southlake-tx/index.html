Water Damage Insurance Claim in Southlake, TX: From First Report to Final Repairs

The order of events after a leak, so you know what happens next and what to keep along the way.

☎ Call (817) 623-9267

By the Southlake Water Damage Pros team · Updated 2026

A water damage insurance claim is a formal request to your insurer to pay for covered damage, and it moves through a fairly predictable sequence. Homeowners in Southlake, TX who know that sequence tend to make fewer rushed decisions on the worst day of the month.

This post follows a claim in time order, from the minute you discover water to the day the last wall is painted. It is general guidance based on Texas Department of Insurance advice, and your own policy has the final word on what is covered.

Hour one: make the house safe and stop the source

Before anything else, shut off the main water valve if the leak is still running. If water is near outlets, appliances, or the electrical panel, stay away and call the utility or 911 first. Gas smell means leave the house.

Once it is safe, lift valuables off wet floors and keep children and pets out of standing water. Turn off power to wet areas only if you can reach the panel dry. This stage is about preventing a bad day from becoming a worse one, not about cleanup.

Day one: report the loss to your insurer

Call the number on your policy or use the insurer's claim app, and report the damage the same day if possible. TDI notes that mold can begin growing within a day after a leak, and for hidden water damage you usually must report within days after you first see it. Ask your insurer for the exact reporting window.

Have your policy number, the time you noticed the problem, and a short description of the cause ready. You will be given a claim number. Write it at the top of a notebook where every call, name, and date goes from here on.

Documentation: what to capture before repairs

Take photos or video of every affected room before anything is moved or repaired, as TDI recommends. Capture the source, the water line, ceiling stains, and ruined belongings. Keep damaged items until the adjuster has seen them.

The checklist below keeps the evidence organized so nothing gets lost in the scramble.

  • Wide and close photos of every wet room, with the date visible if you can.
  • A written inventory of damaged contents with brand and approximate age.
  • The failed part itself, such as a cracked supply line, set aside in a bag.
  • Receipts for every emergency purchase, hotel night, and service call.
  • A running log of who you spoke to at the insurer and what was said.

Temporary repairs versus permanent repairs

Your policy generally expects you to limit further damage. That means tarping a leaking roof, shutting off the line, and removing standing water. These are temporary repairs, and TDI advises making them and keeping the receipts.

Permanent repairs are a different matter. Hold off on replacing drywall, flooring, or cabinets until the insurer has inspected. A crew can still begin water mitigation right away, because drying and extraction protect the house and are part of limiting the loss.

Week one: inspection, drying, and the scope of work

An adjuster will schedule an inspection, and the drying crew keeps working in the meantime. Moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras track where water moved, while air movers and LGR dehumidifiers pull it out of walls and floors. Daily readings show whether materials are actually drying or just feeling dry.

When the water is controlled, the crew writes a scope of work listing what must be cleaned, dried, removed, or rebuilt. Sharing that document with the insurer helps everyone see the same damage. It does not promise payment, and the insurer alone decides what is covered.

For routine cleanup of wet, but sanitary, materials, water damage cleanup covers removal and drying in more detail.

Week two and beyond: repairs, supplements, and mold

Once the insurer has inspected and the structure is dry, rebuild work can begin. Drywall, insulation, flooring, and paint are replaced, usually in that order. If hidden damage turns up behind a wall, tell the insurer before continuing so the added work can be reviewed.

Most policies do not cover gradual leaks or the mold they cause, though mold that develops on a covered damaged item is covered. If mold appears, TDI says to use a state-licensed mold professional, and the insurer will want proof it was removed and the cause fixed. Our mold remediation page explains how that process works.

Delays and disagreements: staying organized

Claims sometimes slow down. The adjuster may be busy after a storm, or a decision may take longer than you hoped. Follow up in writing, keep your log current, and ask the insurer to explain any denial or partial decision in plain terms.

If you disagree, point to your photos, the drying logs, and the scope of work. Never state or assume a payout amount until the insurer confirms it in writing.

A scope of work that follows the published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration gives the adjuster something concrete to review. Ask any company whether its crews work to S500 and whether its scope lists readings, equipment and dates.

Claim logistics for homes near Southlake Town Square and beyond

Southlake Town Square sits at State Highway 114 and Southlake Boulevard, a handy landmark when you give an adjuster or crew directions to a house on the north side of the city. Use the street address first and the landmark second.

If the water came from outside, the city's Streets and Drainage Team responds to storm damage on public property, but city crews may not use public resources to clean private property. Homeowners and HOAs maintain their own property, so cleanup of a private yard or home falls to you and your insurer.
