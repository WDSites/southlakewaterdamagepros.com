Flood Water Cleanup in Southlake, TX: Safe First Steps and What Insurance Covers

Floodwater is not the same as a pipe leak, and the cleanup and the paperwork both work differently.

☎ Call (817) 623-9267

By the Southlake Water Damage Pros team · Updated 2026

Flood water cleanup is the process of removing outside water that has entered a building, then sanitizing, drying, and clearing out whatever cannot be saved. In Southlake, TX it can start with a storm, a clogged drain, or a creek that rises faster than anyone expected.

Floodwater carries soil and contamination, so the rules differ from a clean supply line leak. This guide covers what to do first, how crews sort materials, why insurance treats flooding separately, and how to avoid common mistakes.

Safety before cleanup: what to do while you wait

Stay out of standing floodwater if you can. It may hide sharp debris and may be energized if outlets, cords, or appliances are submerged. If the water is near the electrical panel or you smell gas, leave and call the utility or 911 before you call us.

If it is safe, shut off the main water valve, lift valuables to higher shelves, and keep children and pets away from the water. Shut off power to wet areas only if you can reach the panel dry. Photograph the water line on walls before anything changes.

Why floodwater is treated as contaminated

Restoration standards sort water into categories. Category 1 comes from a clean source such as a supply line. Category 2 carries some contamination, like a washing machine overflow. Category 3 is grossly contaminated and includes sewage and water that has traveled across the ground.

Floodwater from outside is generally handled as Category 3, because of what it has picked up on the way in. ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published industry standard for water damage restoration, and you can ask any company whether its crews work to it.

How a cleanup crew works through a flooded house

The first job is pulling out the water. Extraction units remove standing water, and wet carpet and pad are cut out and hauled away. Drywall that wicked contaminated water is usually cut above the water line so the wall cavity can be reached.

Next comes cleaning and sanitizing hard surfaces, then structural drying. Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run until moisture meters show materials back at normal levels. HEPA air scrubbers and containment barriers are used when dust or debris would spread to clean areas.

What to keep and what to throw out

Hard, nonporous items such as glass, metal, and some plastics can often be cleaned and sanitized. Porous items like mattresses, upholstered furniture, carpet pad, and particleboard cabinets that soaked in floodwater are usually discarded.

Before you discard anything, photograph it and write down what it was. TDI advises keeping damaged items until the adjuster has seen them, so ask your insurer before the curb pile grows. Set aside valuables, papers, and photos in a clean, dry place for separate treatment.

  • Usually salvageable: solid wood furniture, glass, ceramics, metal tools.
  • Usually discarded: carpet and pad, mattresses, soft furnishings, wet insulation.
  • Needs a decision: electronics, framed art, books, and family documents.

Flood insurance and what a home policy covers

This is the part that surprises people most. Home policies do not cover floods, according to the Texas Department of Insurance, so flood damage needs a separate flood policy. A pipe burst inside the house is another matter and is often covered as sudden and accidental.

If you have flood coverage, report the loss quickly, photograph the water line, keep receipts, and make temporary repairs to limit more damage. Hold permanent repairs until the insurer inspects. Never assume a payout or approval. If you are unsure which kind of policy you hold, call your agent and ask.

Drying, mold watch, and rebuild

Mold can begin growing within a day after water arrives, per TDI, so speed matters even when cleanup feels overwhelming. Drying should begin as soon as the water is out and the structure is safe.

If mold shows up anyway, use a state-licensed mold professional. Texas licenses mold assessors and remediators through the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation, and the insurer will want proof the mold was removed and the cause fixed. Our mold remediation page explains the process.

Rebuild comes last: new drywall, insulation, flooring, and paint once readings confirm the structure is dry. Restoration is not waterproofing, so a repaired house can flood again in another storm.

Flood maps and Grapevine Lake context for Southlake homeowners

The city of Southlake publishes an interactive map with the 100-year and 500-year flood zones under its Environmental tab. Check the city's flood zone map and the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for your address before you decide on flood coverage, since the answer decides whether a storm loss has a policy behind it.

Grapevine Lake, a federal reservoir run by the US Army Corps of Engineers, spans Denton and Tarrant counties. It was authorized for flood control and navigation, with construction from January 1948 to June 1952. In June 2015 flooding closed lake parks and boat ramps, and Tarrant County received a disaster declaration on June 3, 2015. Reporting at the time said homes were largely spared, so treat it as a regional reminder rather than a Southlake event.
