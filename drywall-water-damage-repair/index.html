Wall and Drywall Water Damage Repair in Southlake, TX

Drywall tells you quickly when water has been in the wall. We tell you whether it can be dried or has to be cut out and rebuilt.

☎ Call (817) 623-9267

Drywall water damage is what happens when gypsum board and its paper face soak up water from a leak, a roof opening or a plumbing failure and begin to soften, stain and lose strength. If you need wall and drywall water damage repair in Southlake, TX, this page walks through how the board reacts, how crews decide what to cut, and how the wall is finished.

In a typical Southlake house the interior walls are drywall over wood studs, with insulation in the exterior walls and paint over texture. Water can enter from a supply line, a window, a roof or a ceiling above, and then run down inside the wall.

This page focuses on walls. For ceilings, flooring and the broader rebuild, see water damage repair.

How drywall reacts when it gets wet

Drywall is gypsum between two sheets of paper. The paper wicks water upward, often farther than the visible stain, and the gypsum core turns soft and crumbly.

Surface paint can hide a lot. A wall may look nearly normal while the back face is damp, which is why the first step is a moisture meter and not a putty knife.

Wet drywall that stays wet gives mold a place to start. The Texas Department of Insurance says mold can begin growing within a day after a leak, so the timeline matters more than the stain.

Safety steps in the first hour

Shut off the water at the main valve if a pipe is involved, and call a licensed plumber. If water has reached outlets, switches or light fixtures on the wall, stay out of the room and call the electric utility or 911 before you call us.

Do not push on a bulging wall or ceiling to see how much it holds. Water can be trapped behind it, and the board may drop. For city-side water problems, Southlake Public Works takes after-hours calls at (817) 748-8082.

Move furniture away from the wall, take photos, and open nearby doors. Do not paint over a stain to make it disappear, since that traps moisture and hides the damage from the insurer.

Dry it or cut it: how the decision is made

Not every wet wall has to come apart. Drywall that is firm, was exposed to clean water for a short time and dries fast can sometimes be saved with controlled drying and cavity airflow.

Insulation behind it is a separate question. Fiberglass batts that soak up water hold it and mat down, so they usually come out, while the framing is dried and tested before anything closes.

Cutting above the wet line and opening the cavity

When a wall has to be cut, the cut goes above the wet line, not along it. Moisture usually travels upward in the paper, so a clean line a hand's width or more above the last reading leaves nothing damp behind.

We use a moisture meter along the stud faces to confirm where the dry wall begins. If the baseboard is swollen, it is removed carefully so it can be reused when it is in good shape.

Once the cavity is open, the crew sets air movers and an LGR dehumidifier so dry air reaches the studs and bottom plate. The full dry-out process is described on our structural drying page.

Rebuilding the wall: tape, mud, texture and paint

New drywall goes in only after the framing reads dry. The board is hung, the seams are taped, and joint compound is spread in thin coats and sanded between coats.

  • Hang new board cut to fit the opening, with seams landing on studs where possible.
  • Tape and apply joint compound in thin coats, sanding smooth.
  • Match the existing texture so the patch does not stand out in side light.
  • Prime new board with a sealing primer so the paint dries evenly.
  • Paint the whole wall from corner to corner, not just the patch.
  • Reinstall baseboard, caulk the joints and touch up trim.

Texture matching depends on how the original was applied, and a close match is the goal even when an exact match is not possible.

What changes the cost of wall repair

Wall repair prices follow the scope. Here is what moves it.

  • Number of paint coats and how much of the room must be repainted for a clean finish.

Permits, registration and insurance order of operations

The City of Southlake permit guide lists a remodel of an existing building that involves walls, electric, plumbing or window replacement as needing a permit, while painting is cosmetic. Rebuilding a wall after a leak may fall on either side of that line, so ask the Southlake building inspections office at (817) 748-8236.

Building Inspections also says general contractors register with the city every year and trade contractors with a current state license must register as well. Ask any contractor whether it is registered.

ANSI/IICRC S500 is a published industry standard for water damage restoration, including how wet building materials are dried. Ask any company whether its crews work to it.

The Texas Department of Insurance advises holding permanent repairs until the insurer has inspected. Check your policy, ask your insurer what it needs, and keep photos and receipts for any temporary repairs. We cannot promise approval.

Mistakes to avoid with wet walls

A few common choices turn a small wall repair into a bigger one.

  • Painting over a stain before the wall has been tested.
  • Patching soft drywall with joint compound only.
  • Closing the wall over wet insulation or framing.
  • Skipping primer, which lets the stain bleed through the new paint.
  • Starting permanent repairs before the insurer has inspected.
