Emergency Frozen Pipe Water Damage Restoration in Southlake, TX

The ice is not the damage. The thaw is, and it can open several leaks at the same time.

A pipe froze and now water is coming through a wall, a ceiling or the garage. Our dispatch line is open 24/7. While you wait for help, shut off the water at the main, keep warm air moving toward the cold spot, and keep everyone away from any wet ceiling.

Frozen pipe water damage in Southlake, TX is what happens when water inside a line turns to ice, expands and cracks the pipe or its fittings, then floods the structure once the ice melts and pressure returns. Not every Southlake home has pipes in exposed spots, so the risk depends on how your house was built.

The tricky part is timing. A pipe can hold a crack for hours while it is frozen, and the leak only starts at the thaw. That is why this page is separate from a sudden burst pipe in a warm house.

Thaw day in Southlake: what to do while you wait

Start by finding your main shutoff valve and closing it. Do this before you try to thaw anything. If a cracked pipe is hidden in a wall, closing the main means the thaw will not turn into a flood.

Next, look for the pipe that is frozen. Common places are lines in exterior walls, attics, garages, and the run to an outdoor spigot, all spots where cold air reaches the pipe. If a faucet gives only a trickle or nothing, open it and leave it open so water has a way to move as the ice melts.

Take photos of every wet area, frosted pipe and stain. If it is safe to reach your panel without stepping in water, switch off power to wet rooms. If the ceiling is bowed or the water is near outlets, leave and call the electric utility or 911 first.

Reading the signs of a pipe that froze

The City of Southlake's winter pipe guidance lists low or no water pressure, frost on exposed pipes, and water spots or pooling as warning signs. Those clues often show up before the thaw, and they tell you where to look.

This table connects what you notice to what may be happening inside the structure.

Several breaks can open in a single event, one in the attic and another behind the garage wall. After a freeze, assume there may be more than one wet area until the readings say otherwise.

Safety steps for thawing and restoring water

The City of Southlake advises thawing a frozen pipe with warm air, such as a hair dryer or a space heater, and never an open flame. Never leave a heater running unattended, and keep it clear of anything that burns.

Once the ice is gone, do not open the main all the way at once. The city recommends restoring water slowly and checking for leaks as it returns. Open it a little, listen, walk the house, look at ceilings and cabinet bases, then open further.

A water emergency inside the home calls for a licensed plumber. City-side water concerns go to Public Works at (817) 748-8082. Call a plumber to repair the split sections, and call us for the water that already spread.

How the dry-out works after a freeze

Frozen pipe jobs often involve areas people do not see. The crew checks the attic, the wall behind a garage, the cabinet bases and the ceiling below any upstairs line with moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras, then maps the wet zones before pulling anything out.

Water that came from a clean supply line is usually category 1, which is easier to dry in place if it is caught early. Extraction units pull standing water, then air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry what is left. Wet insulation is removed, and drywall is opened only where it will not recover.

Insulation in an attic can hold water long after the ceiling looks dry. For the stage after drying, see our water damage repair page, and for stopping spread right away, see water mitigation.

What shapes the cost after a freeze

Scope drives price more than any single number, and these are the factors that move it.

  • How many separate leaks opened at the thaw.
  • Whether the water was clean or sat long enough to change category.
  • Whether it spread to ceilings, attic insulation or lower floors.
  • Which materials got wet, from drywall to cabinets and flooring.
  • How many drying days the readings call for.
  • How much rebuild is needed after the structure is dry.

Insurance, the 2021 storm and who owns the line

The February 2021 winter storm tested North Texas plumbing, and plenty of homeowners learned how a freeze turns into a claim. Many policies cover sudden and accidental water damage, but check your policy and ask your insurer how it treats freeze damage in your case. Photos before repairs and saved receipts help.

If the leak is outside the house, ownership matters. The city's own website says residents are responsible for the service line from the house to and including the tap, and that if part of the line lies under the road, the City repairs that section. Contact the Wastewater Division before calling a plumber for a line outside your walls.

Prevention before the next hard freeze

The City of Southlake publishes a list for protecting pipes in cold weather, and it is worth following. Drain outdoor sprinkler systems, disconnect hoses and cover outdoor faucets. Seal drafts and insulate exposed pipes in garages, attics, crawl spaces or exterior walls.

Know where your main shutoff is. In extended freezing weather, open cabinet doors under sinks, let faucets drip slightly, and keep the thermostat at 55 degrees F or higher even when you are away.

Published standards such as ANSI/IICRC S500 describe how water damage is dried, and you can ask any company whether its crews work to it. We serve Southlake and nearby towns, including Colleyville and Roanoke.
