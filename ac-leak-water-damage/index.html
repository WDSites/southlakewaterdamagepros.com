AC Leak Water Damage Repair in Southlake, TX

When condensate backs up from an air handler, it stains ceilings and cups floors, and we dry and repair both.

☎ Call (817) 623-9267

AC leak water damage repair in Southlake, TX covers the drying and rebuilding after an air conditioner or its drain line releases water into a home. Cooling coils pull moisture out of the air, and that water is meant to leave through a condensate drain line.

When that line clogs, the drain pan overflows or the pan itself cracks, the water goes where gravity sends it. If the air handler sits in an attic, it drips onto the ceiling below. If it sits in a closet, it soaks the floor beside it and often runs under the closet door into hardwood.

Not every Southlake home has an attic unit, and the layout depends on how your house was built. Check where your air handler lives before you decide what to look for.

Reading the signs of a condensate leak

AC water is clean at first, but it travels through dusty insulation and sits in pans, so it picks up grime quickly. These are the usual signs and what they tend to mean.

SignWhat it usually meansWhere we look
Brown ring or sag on a ceilingOverflow from an attic air handler or drain panCeiling drywall, attic insulation, joists
Water in the pan under the unitClogged drain line or failed float switchPan, drain line, nearby framing
Hardwood cupping near a closetSeepage from a closet air handlerCloset floor, planks, subfloor edges
Unit shuts off by itselfFloat switch tripped by a full panPan, line, thermostat history
Damp smell near a vent or closetSlow leak inside a cabinet or wallCavity readings, baseboards

A heating and cooling technician confirms the fault. Our job begins with the wet building materials around it.

Safety steps when the air handler is dripping

Turn the thermostat to off first. That stops the system from making more condensate and removes the load from the unit. Then switch off the unit's breaker if the panel is dry and easy to reach.

Keep your distance from any light fixture or fan that is getting water through the ceiling. A bulging ceiling can release a lot of water at once, so move people and furniture out from under it and do not poke it from below.

Do not climb into a wet attic to chase the leak. Insulation hides the joists, and a step in the wrong place can go through the ceiling. A licensed HVAC technician can check the unit safely, and we handle the water that has already moved into the home.

  • Set the thermostat to off and cut power to the unit at the breaker.
  • Place a bucket under active drips and move valuables out of the room.
  • Shop-vac the pan only if the unit is off and you are on a dry surface.
  • Photograph the pan, the line and every stain before cleaning anything.
  • Call a licensed HVAC technician for the drain line and float switch.

How we dry a ceiling, closet or floor after an AC leak

In an attic leak, wet insulation holds water like a sponge against the ceiling. We remove the saturated portion, extract what we can reach, and cut out ceiling drywall that has lost its strength. Airflow is set up so the joists dry too.

In a closet leak, the water goes down and out. We use an extraction unit on hard floors, lift baseboards and place air movers at the closet threshold. For hardwood, small drying mats and low-profile dehumidifiers pull moisture out of the planks. Cupped boards sometimes settle back when dried slowly, though some planks may need replacing.

A moisture meter and a thermal imaging camera mark the edges of the wet area, and we recheck readings until they reach the dry reference points. The published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard is a reference for this work, and you can ask any company whether its crews work to it.

Why slow AC leaks are an insurance problem

A clogged condensate line often drips for weeks before anyone sees a stain. The Texas Department of Insurance says most policies do not cover gradual leaks or seepage, including the mold they cause. Mold can also begin within a day after a leak, according to the same agency, so a slow leak can build up damage in two ways.

That does not mean every AC claim is denied. A sudden overflow or a cracked pan may be treated differently from a long seep. Check your policy, ask your insurer how it classifies the event, and report it quickly once you see it.

Take photos and video before cleanup, keep the damaged items until the adjuster has seen them, and save receipts for anything you buy. We record readings and the wet area so your adjuster has real information, and we never promise coverage.

Who handles what: HVAC tech, contractor and us

Three kinds of work may be involved. The HVAC technician clears or replaces the drain line, the pan and the float switch. We dry and remove wet materials. A rebuild contractor then restores the ceiling or floor.

The City of Southlake Building Inspections page says trade contractors with a current state license need to register with the city. If a contractor is replacing equipment or doing work that needs a permit, ask whether they are registered, and call Building Inspections at (817) 748-8236 with questions.

If the damage includes water-stained ceilings or floors, our water damage repair page explains the rebuild stage. For a bigger area with standing water, emergency water extraction covers the pump-out stage.

What changes the cost after an AC leak

Price depends on what the water touched and how long it sat. The factors below set the scope before any figure is possible.

  • How long the line was overflowing before anyone saw it.
  • Location of the air handler, attic or closet, and what sits below it.
  • Whether ceiling drywall and insulation must be removed.
  • Flooring type, especially hardwood that needs slow drying.
  • Number of drying days and pieces of equipment needed.

Preventing the next condensate backup

Most AC leaks repeat if the cause is not addressed. Ask your HVAC technician about flushing the drain line, adding a float switch or cleaning the pan. These are service choices for a licensed technician, not something we install.

Look at the pan and line at the start of the cooling season, change filters on schedule, and watch for a unit that keeps shutting off. Even a quick look at the closet floor can catch a drip early.
