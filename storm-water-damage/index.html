Emergency Storm Water Damage Cleanup in Southlake, TX

When wind, hail or a fallen limb lets rain into your home, we dry the rooms it reached.

☎ Call (817) 623-9267

A storm just passed and your house is wet. Our dispatch line is open 24/7 at (817) 623-9267. While you wait, stay away from downed power lines, move belongings away from windows and ceilings, and keep rain out with a tarp or board if you can do it safely from inside.

Storm water damage in Southlake, TX is interior water damage from rain that arrives from above or through an opening: wind-driven rain through a window, hail that breaks shingles, a limb through the roof or gutters that overflow into a wall. It is different from a flood, where water rises from the ground.

That difference matters for the cleanup and for your insurance policy.

Right after the storm passes

Start with people and power. Walk around the outside only when it is calm, and treat every downed line as live. Call 911 for lines, fires or injuries, and call your electric utility to report damage to the service drop.

Inside, put out pans and towels, lift valuables off wet floors and take photos before you move anything. If water is pooled on the floor, remove standing water with a mop or wet vacuum when the room is safe to enter. The Texas Department of Insurance advises homeowners to cover broken windows and holes to keep more rain from coming in.

Save every receipt for tarps, plywood and supplies. A policy may reimburse temporary repairs.

Safety steps for the first hour of storm damage

Safety comes before cleanup. Stay out of any room with water near outlets, a light fixture or the breaker panel. If your panel is wet or you smell gas, leave the house and call 911 and your utility before you call us.

Do not walk through attic spaces with wet insulation, and do not stand under a ceiling that droops. Broken glass and nails hide in debris, so wear boots and gloves. If a tree is on the roof, leave it alone and let professionals remove it.

  • Treat every downed line as live and stay far away.
  • Turn off power to wet rooms only if the panel is dry and easy to reach.
  • Wear sturdy shoes and gloves around broken glass and debris.
  • Keep people and pets out of rooms with sagging ceilings.
  • Call 911 for fire, gas odor, sparks or injury.

How we dry a storm-soaked home

Storm water usually comes from rain, so it is cleaner than sewage or creek water, but it picks up dirt, insulation fibers and roof grit on the way in. A crew checks for contamination first, then maps the wet areas with moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera.

Standing water comes out with an extraction unit. Soaked insulation and ceiling board are removed, and wet baseboards are pulled so walls can breathe. Air movers push dry air across floors and walls while LGR dehumidifiers pull the moisture out of the room.

Carpet and pad are lifted and dried when they can be saved, and replaced when they cannot. Our emergency water extraction service covers pumping out larger volumes, and water mitigation explains how we stop the spread early.

We work to the published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, and you should ask any company whether its crews work to it.

Storm damage or flood damage

Insurance companies treat these differently, so describe the water honestly. Rain through the roof or a broken window is storm damage. Water that rises from the ground, a creek or a street is flood damage.

Where does the water come from?Above or through an openingRising from the ground or outside

The Texas Department of Insurance says home and renters policies do not cover flood. If your water came up from the ground, a separate flood policy is what applies. Ask your insurer how your claim is classified.

What changes the cost of storm cleanup

Storm jobs vary widely, so we explain scope and price before work starts. These are the factors that move it.

  • How many rooms the water reached and whether it went through ceilings.
  • Whether insulation, drywall, carpet or pad must be removed.
  • The water category after we test and look at what the water carried.
  • Drying days and how much equipment the structure needs.
  • Rebuild scope after drying, which is priced apart from mitigation.

Filing the claim without losing ground

The Texas Department of Insurance advises filing as soon as you can and photographing damage inside and outside, including video. Keep damaged items until the insurer says you may discard them, and be present when the adjuster visits.

The same guidance says to get more than one bid on repairs, never pay the full amount upfront, and never sign a contract with blank spaces. State law makes it illegal to waive a homeowners deductible, so avoid anyone who offers to. If you cannot stay home, most policies cover some related living costs, so keep receipts.

Drainage, gutters and the lake in the background

The city's own website says its Streets and Drainage Team responds to storm damage, but city crews are not allowed to use public resources to clean private property. Keeping your own gutters, downspouts and yard drains clear is your job or your homeowners association's.

Grapevine Lake sits nearby as a federal flood-control reservoir built by the US Army Corps of Engineers. That does not mean Southlake floods, but it explains why checking the city's flood zone map and the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for your address is worth doing. Roof damage during the same storm is covered on our roof leak page.

Mistakes after a storm

Do not throw away damaged items before the adjuster sees them. Do not hire anyone who knocks on your door after a storm and asks for money upfront. Do not leave wet drywall in place hoping it will dry.

Mold can begin growing within a day after water gets in, according to the Texas Department of Insurance, so a fast response limits what is lost. We serve Keller and Roanoke too, with the same drying process.
