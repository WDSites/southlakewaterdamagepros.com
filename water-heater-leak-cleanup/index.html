Emergency Water Heater Leak Cleanup in Southlake, TX

Hot water, a power or gas supply and a closet wall make this one worth handling carefully.

☎ Call (817) 623-9267

Water is pooling around your water heater, and it may be hot. Our dispatch line is open 24/7. While you wait, stay out of the standing water, shut off the cold water supply to the heater, and switch off its power or gas only if you can do it without stepping into the puddle.

Water heater leak cleanup in Southlake, TX is the removal and drying of water that escaped from a tank, a connection or a relief valve into the space around it. Where the heater sits matters, since some homes have it in a garage, some in a closet and some on an attic platform over finished ceilings.

The tank can hold a lot of water, and a failed tank can release it fast. This page explains the safe sequence, who handles the unit, and how the closet, floor and ceilings below get dried.

Leaking water heater right now: what to do first

First, cut the supply to the tank. There is a cold water valve on the pipe feeding the heater, and turning it clockwise stops new water from entering. If the valve is stuck or the leak is heavy, close the home's main shutoff instead.

Next, deal with the energy source. An electric heater runs on the breaker panel, and a gas heater has a gas control valve on the unit. Only touch the breaker if the panel is dry and you do not have to cross water. If you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 before you call us.

Water from a heater can be hot enough to scald, so keep children and pets away. Photograph the unit, the puddle and the surrounding walls before anything is moved.

Where the water comes from and what it means

Not every wet spot at a heater means a dead tank. Sometimes the fix is a fitting, and sometimes the whole unit is done. A plumber makes that call, and this table shows what each clue tends to point to.

What you seeLikely sourceWhat it means
Drip from the pipe at the side or topRelief valve or discharge lineMay be a valve problem or too much pressure
Wet connections on top of the tankSupply fittingsOften repairable by a plumber
Steady puddle under the tankLeak at the base or from the tank itselfUsually means replacement
Rust-colored water or rusted tankCorrosion in the tankThe unit is likely at the end of its life
Stain on the ceiling belowLeak from an upper-level or attic heaterWater has crossed the floor and entered the ceiling

Where a heater sits determines how far the water travels. A garage slab lets water run toward a drain, while a closet with finished walls lets it soak baseboards and flooring. An attic platform can send water straight through the ceiling below.

Safety steps before anyone touches the unit

Water and electricity do not mix, so treat a wet heater cabinet and any wet outlets nearby as live until a professional says otherwise. Do not stand in water to reach the breaker, and do not use a household vacuum on the puddle.

Do not open the drain valve or the pressure relief valve to see what happens. That can release scalding water. If you have to move around the heater, wear shoes with grip.

Call a licensed plumber to inspect, repair or replace the unit. For city-side water concerns, Public Works can be reached at (817) 748-8082. Once the source is closed, we can dry the area without waiting for the new tank.

Who handles the heater and who handles the water

A plumber replaces or repairs the water heater. We handle the water that left it. Splitting the work this way keeps both jobs moving, and neither crew has to guess about the other's tools.

If you are replacing the heater, the City of Southlake permit guide says a permit is required for replacing or adding a water heater. Ask your plumber who pulls it, and ask the Southlake building inspections office at (817) 748-8236 if you are unsure, as the list in the guide is not all-inclusive. Your homeowners association covenants can add their own rules.

The City's Building Inspections page also notes that trade contractors with a current state license must register with the city. Ask any contractor you hire whether they are registered.

How we dry a closet, garage or ceiling after a heater leak

Extraction units pull the standing water first. Then moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera show how far it traveled, including into the base of the walls and under flooring.

In a closet, baseboards often come off, and small flood cuts in the drywall let the cavity breathe. Air movers push dry air across the surfaces and LGR dehumidifiers pull moisture from the air. In a garage, the slab dries fast, but any wall touching it still needs checking.

If the heater sits above living space, the ceiling below is the priority. Wet drywall and insulation are checked, and what cannot be dried is cut out. When a leak is more of a spread across the room, our water mitigation approach covers the stop-the-spread work.

What shapes the cost of a heater leak cleanup

The size of the tank matters less than where the water went. These are the factors that set the scope.

  • How long the water ran before the supply was closed.
  • Whether it stayed in a garage or reached carpet, hardwood or drywall.
  • Whether a ceiling below was soaked.
  • Whether the leak was slow and long term, which can mean more hidden damage.
  • How many drying days and how much equipment the readings call for.
  • How much rebuild follows after the structure is dry.

Insurance and mistakes to avoid

The Texas Department of Insurance says most homeowners policies cover water damage from leaks and broken pipes, with exceptions. Check your policy and ask your insurer how it treats a heater failure. A slow leak that went on for months may be treated differently from a sudden one, so ask.

The same source says an insurer may deny a claim if you do not protect your property, so shut off the supply and move valuables off the floor. Keep the failed unit if the adjuster wants to see it, and photograph the label before the plumber hauls it away.

Common mistakes include running the unit dry after a leak, mopping and walking away, and letting a plumber rip out wet material before anyone has taken photos. Published standards such as ANSI/IICRC S500 describe water damage drying, and you can ask any company whether its crews work to it.
