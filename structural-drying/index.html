Structural Drying and Dehumidification in Southlake, TX

We find where the moisture went, pull it out of framing and subfloor, and keep measuring until the readings say the house is dry.

☎ Call (817) 623-9267

Structural drying is the controlled removal of moisture from the framing, subfloor, drywall and insulation that soaked up water, using air movers and dehumidifiers that are placed and checked on purpose. If you need structural drying in Southlake, TX, this page explains what the crew measures, which machines go where, and why the job takes days instead of an afternoon.

Water that has been pumped out is not the same as a dry house. Wood, gypsum and concrete hold water inside them, and a closed-up home with the air conditioning running can leave that moisture sitting in the walls long after the surface feels dry to the touch.

This is the in-depth drying stage. If water is still running or standing, start with water mitigation to stop the spread, then come back here for how the dry-out is actually carried out and proven.

Why a floor that feels dry can still be wet

Your hand can only judge the surface. A subfloor under vinyl, the bottom plate of an interior wall, and the back face of drywall can all stay wet while the paint and flooring above them feel normal.

Moisture also moves from wet materials into the drier ones beside them. That is why a wall that took on water at the baseboard can keep carrying it upward after the puddle is gone.

Texas summers add a second problem. Warm, humid air holds a lot of water, so a house that is shut tight with the thermostat set low can hold the damp in place instead of clearing it. Drying works by taking water out of the building, not by hoping the weather does the job.

Safety steps before drying equipment goes in

Wet rooms and electricity do not mix. If water reached outlets, a panel or anything plugged in, leave the area and call the electric utility or 911 before you call us. Do not stand in water to flip a breaker.

If the source is still active, shut off the main water valve and call a licensed plumber. For water concerns on the city side of the meter, Southlake Public Works takes after-hours calls at (817) 748-8082. Move rugs, electronics and paper off wet floors, and keep children and pets out of the room.

Our drying process, step by step

Every dry-out starts with a map. The crew walks the affected rooms with a moisture meter, marks the wet boundary, and records baseline readings from a dry area of the same material so there is a target to reach.

  • Moisture mapping: meters and a thermal imaging camera find the wet edge and hidden pockets.
  • Removal of what cannot dry: soaked padding, loose insulation and ruined board come out so the rest can dry fast.
  • Air movers: low, wide fans aimed along wall bases and across floors to pull moisture off surfaces.
  • LGR dehumidifiers: low grain refrigerant machines that pull water vapor out of the air so it can take more from the materials.
  • Wall cavity drying: small holes or lifted baseboards let dry air reach the space behind drywall, and injection drying pushes air into closed cavities.
  • HEPA air filtration where dust or debris is stirred up during demolition.
  • Monitoring: daily or near daily visits to re-read the same points and adjust the equipment.

We do not rent equipment out for you to run alone. We place it, check it and take it away, because the readings are the proof that drying is done.

Why drying takes days and what happens each visit

The first day removes the standing water and sets the machines. After that, the crew returns to read the same marked points. If a reading has stopped falling, the equipment is moved, a cavity is opened, or another dehumidifier is added.

Dense materials such as subfloor, framing and concrete release moisture slowly, so they set the schedule. The job is done when the slowest material reaches the dry reference reading.

A published industry standard, ANSI/IICRC S500, describes professional water damage restoration practice, including drying goals and documentation. Ask any company whether its crews work to it, and ask to see the readings that show the job reached the target.

Mold timing, paperwork and who does the work

The Texas Department of Insurance says mold can begin growing within a day after a leak. That is the practical reason to start drying early and to keep the equipment running until readings are normal, instead of shutting it off when the room feels fine.

The same insurance agency advises photographing the damage before repairs and keeping damaged items until the adjuster has seen them. Moisture logs from the dry-out give you dated records to hand over, along with photos of the wet area before anything is cut.

The City of Southlake says general contractors register with the city every year and trade contractors with a current state license must register as well. If your job needs a licensed plumber or electrician, ask whether that trade is registered with Building Inspections.

How insurance usually fits

Texas policies commonly respond to sudden and accidental water damage and often do not respond to slow leaks or seepage. Drying costs are part of limiting further damage, but check your policy for what your carrier counts as covered.

Ask your insurer how it wants the dry-out documented. We cannot promise any claim will be approved.

Mistakes that leave a house damp

Most failed dry-outs come from stopping early, because the surface looks fine and the household wants its space back.

  • Turning off equipment before the crew has re-read the marked points.
  • Painting or closing walls over cavities that were never tested.
  • Opening windows on a humid day and letting more moisture in.
  • Covering a damp floor with new flooring too soon.
  • Running only fans with no dehumidifier, which moves water around the room instead of out of it.
